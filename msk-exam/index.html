General exam

Week 1 · every joint

Approach to every joint

  • Look (inspection): drape so both sides can be seen, look from all sides
    • general impression: body habitus, distress, signs of systemic disease
    • SEADS: Swelling/Scars · Erythema · Atrophy/Alignment · Deformity · Symmetry
  • Feel (palpation): temperature side to side (warmth = inflammation), swelling, then name each bony landmark, ligament, tendon and bursa as you press it, watching the patient's face
  • Move (range of motion, ROM)
    • active first (the patient moves it): if limited, is it weakness, pain or something else?
    • then passive (you move it): feel for crepitus and the end feel
    • active limited but passive full → tendon or nerve problem · both limited → the joint itself (arthritis, capsule)
  • Special tests: chosen for the joint and the suspected problem (sections below)
  • Neurovascular screen: sensation, strength, reflexes · pulses and skin
    • arterial disease: cold, pale, weak pulses, shiny hairless skin
    • venous disease: warm, brown/purple, pitting edema, varicose veins
  • Gait: always part of a back or lower limb exam
  • Adapting the exam: for a patient with a physical disability, modify the position or the instructions but still get the information that makes the diagnosis

Gait

Watch barefoot, from the front, back and side, at the patient's own speed. Add a functional screen: walk on toes (S1), walk on heels (L4-L5), tandem walk (balance), squat (proximal weakness).

GaitWhat you seeWhy
AntalgicShorter stance time on the painful sidePain in the leg: knee arthritis, ankle sprain
TrendelenburgUncompensated: pelvis drops on the opposite side during stance · Compensated: trunk lurches over the weak side, pelvis stays levelWeak gluteus medius (hip abductor): hip pain, gluteal tendon tear, L5 radiculopathy · both sides weak → waddling gait
Foot dropFoot slaps down after heel strike, or toes touch first · compensates with high hip flexion, circumduction or hip hikingWeak ankle dorsiflexors: L4/L5 radiculopathy, sciatic or peroneal nerve injury
AtaxicUneven step length and width, wide baseCerebellar lesion, cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression)

Step length = one foot to the opposite foot · stride length = one foot to the same foot again.

Cervical spine

Week 1 · 2 special tests

General exam

Look · Feel · Move

  • Look: normal cervical lordosis, forward head posture or head tilt, wasting of trapezius, deltoid and the arm muscles, scars
  • Feel: occiput, spinous processes, sternocleidomastoid, paraspinal muscles, lymph nodes, thyroid, carotid pulse
  • Move: flexion 0-90° · extension 0-90° · rotation 0-90° · lateral flexion 0-45°
  • Also examine the shoulder and thoracic spine, and watch gait for spasticity or ataxia (cervical myelopathy)

Lumbar spine

Week 1 · 7 special tests

General exam

Look · Feel · Move

  • Look: lordosis (increased or flattened), scoliosis, iliac crests level, paraspinal wasting, scars, signs of spinal dysraphism (hairy patch, sacral dimple), café-au-lait spots, fasciculations
  • Feel: spinous processes, iliac crests, posterior superior iliac spine (PSIS), sacroiliac (SI) joints, ischial tuberosity, greater trochanter, paraspinal and gluteal muscles, piriformis
  • Move: flexion, extension, lateral flexion, rotation: smooth and symmetric? Look for scoliosis on forward bend (Adam's forward bend test)
  • Also examine the hip and thoracic spine; watch gait for antalgia or foot drop
  • Work in one position at a time: standing → sitting → lying on the back → side or front

General exam

Look · Feel · Move

  • Look: swelling at the acromioclavicular (AC), sternoclavicular (SC) and glenohumeral (GH) joints · squaring of the shoulder (deltoid wasting), wasting over the scapula (supraspinatus, infraspinatus) · anterior dislocation, clavicle deformity, scapular winging · scars

Elbow

Week 2 · 4 special tests

General exam

Look · Feel · Move

  • Look: loss of the grooves beside the olecranon, swelling over the radial head, olecranon bursa fullness · carrying angle (varus or valgus), flexion contracture · forearm wasting
  • Feel: warmth vs the other elbow · radial head, epicondyles, olecranon · extensor insertion (lateral epicondyle), flexor insertion (medial epicondyle), triceps, collateral ligaments, ulnar nerve in the cubital tunnel
  • Move: extension 0-5° · flexion 145° · pronation 90° · supination 90°
  • A swollen elbow is held bent and won't straighten fully. Tell joint swelling from olecranon bursitis (a bursa does not limit movement).
  • Also examine the shoulder and wrist; think of cervical radiculopathy

Wrist and hand

Week 2 · 4 special tests

General exam

Look · Feel · Move

  • Look: swelling at the distal (DIP), proximal (PIP) and metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joints, the thumb base (1st carpometacarpal, CMC) and tendon sheaths · thenar and hypothenar wasting · Heberden's (DIP) and Bouchard's (PIP) nodes · ulnar drift, swan neck, boutonnière, Z-thumb · rheumatoid nodules · carpal tunnel scar
  • Feel: ulnar styloid, distal radio-ulnar joint, scaphoid (anatomic snuffbox), hook of hamate, metacarpals · thumb tendons at the wrist (EPB and APL, the extensor pollicis brevis and abductor pollicis longus), nodules in the palm
  • Move: wrist extension 0-80° · flexion 0-80° · pronation and supination 90° · ulnar deviation 0-30° · radial deviation 0-20° · full fist and full finger extension
  • Also examine the elbow and cervical spine

Pearls and X-rays

Remember

  • The 1st CMC is the hand's most common osteoarthritis site (weak grip, pain at the thumb base); OA also hits DIPs and PIPs.
  • Numb thumb and index finger is not always carpal tunnel: it may be a C6 radiculopathy.

Hip

Week 3 · 7 special tests

General exam

Look · Feel · Move

  • Look: hip joint swelling cannot be seen · redness over the trochanteric bursa · gluteal wasting · flexion contracture, increased lumbar lordosis, pelvic tilt, leg alignment hip to toe · lateral scar (hip replacement or fracture repair)
  • Feel: greater trochanter, pubic symphysis, iliac crest, anterior and posterior superior iliac spines (ASIS, PSIS), sacrum, SI joint, ischial tuberosity · inguinal nodes, femoral artery, gluteals, piriformis, iliotibial (IT) band
  • Move: flexion 135° · external rotation 45° · internal rotation 35° (lost first in hip arthritis, then flexion) · abduction 45° · adduction 20°
  • Also examine the knee and lumbar spine, and gait
  • True hip joint pain is felt in the groin. Lateral thigh and buttock pain usually is not the hip joint.

Knee

Week 3 · 12 special tests

General exam

Look · Feel · Move

  • Look: loss of the grooves beside the patella, fullness above the patella, Baker's cyst (behind the knee), prepatellar bursa · quadriceps wasting · varus, valgus, flexion contracture, hyperextension (genu recurvatum) · scars
  • Feel: warmth vs the other knee · effusion: wipe (bulge) test for small, ballottement for moderate to large · femoral condyles, joint lines, patella, fibular head, tibial tubercle · collateral ligaments, popliteal fossa, pes anserine bursa, IT band, quadriceps and patellar tendons, hamstrings
  • Move: full extension to 135° flexion
  • Also examine the hip and ankle, and gait
  • ⚠ Always compare a ligament with the other knee: not every loose ligament is abnormal.

Fracture: X-ray or not?

Ottawa ankle and foot rules

Who needs an X-ray (age 18 or over)

Steps
  1. Press the back edge and tip of each malleolus (distal 6 cm of tibia and fibula)
  2. Press the base of the 5th metatarsal and the navicular
  3. Ask them to walk 4 steps
Positive

Tap to reveal

Pearls and X-rays

Remember

Ottawa ankle and foot rules (age ≥ 18): X-ray if there is bony tenderness at the distal 6 cm of the tibia or fibula (back edge or tip of either malleolus), the base of the 5th metatarsal or the navicular, or the patient cannot bear weight for 4 steps both right after the injury and in the emergency department.

  • Most "bunions" are osteoarthritis of the 1st MTP.
  • Cannot rise onto the toes on one side → think tibialis posterior tendon rupture.
  • Flat foot: rigid or supple? Rise onto the toes: the arch returns = supple. Flat feet cause problems further up (knee, hip, back).
  • X-rays: AP, lateral and mortise view (the ankle joint without the fibula overlapping).
