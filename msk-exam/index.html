Cervical spine

Week 1 · 2 tests

Nerve root compression (radiculopathy)

Spurling's maneuver

Cervical nerve root compression (radiculopathy)

Steps
  1. Patient sits
  2. Extend the neck and turn the head toward the painful side
  3. Press gently straight down on top of the head
Positive

Shooting pain or tingling down the arm (neck pain alone does not count)

Tap to reveal

Cervical distraction

Nerve root compression

Steps
  1. Patient lies on their back (or sits)
  2. Cup the back of the head and the chin
  3. Gently lift the head to pull the neck long
Positive

Arm symptoms ease

Tap to reveal

Lumbar spine

Week 1 · 7 tests

Low lumbar root (L5/S1): disc herniation

Straight leg raise (Lasègue)

L5/S1 nerve root tension (sciatic nerve), usually a disc herniation

Steps
  1. Patient lies on their back
  2. Hold the heel and lift the leg with the knee straight
  3. Note the angle where symptoms start
Positive

Pain shooting below the knee between 30° and 70° · back pain only = negative

Tap to reveal

Bragard's sign

Confirms a positive straight leg raise

Steps
  1. Raise the leg until the pain starts
  2. Lower it just until the pain goes
  3. Dorsiflex the ankle (pull the toes up)
Positive

Leg pain returns

Tap to reveal

Bowstring sign

Confirms a positive straight leg raise

Steps
  1. Raise the leg until the pain starts
  2. Bend the knee slightly until the pain eases
  3. Press on the nerve behind the knee (popliteal fossa)
Positive

Leg pain returns

Tap to reveal

Crossed straight leg raise

Disc herniation

Steps
  1. Patient lies on their back
  2. Lift the unaffected (pain-free) leg straight
Positive

Pain in the affected leg (very specific for a disc herniation)

Tap to reveal

Upper lumbar root (L2-L4)

Femoral stretch test

Upper lumbar roots (L2-L4)

Steps
  1. Patient lies face down
  2. Bend the knee, bringing the heel toward the buttock
  3. Optionally lift the thigh to extend the hip
Positive

Pain in the front of the thigh

Tap to reveal

Inflammatory back disease

Modified Schober's test

Inflammatory back disease (e.g. ankylosing spondylitis) restricting lumbar flexion

Steps
  1. Patient stands; mark the spine between the dimples of Venus (lower back dimples)
  2. Mark 10 cm above it
  3. Patient bends forward, knees straight, and you remeasure
Positive

Distance increases by less than 5 cm

Tap to reveal

Scoliosis

Scoliosis

Positive

Impingement (rotator cuff)

Neer's test

Impingement

Steps

Impingement

Elbow

Week 2 · 4 tests

Collateral ligaments

Valgus and varus stress

Medial (ulnar) collateral and lateral collateral ligaments

Steps
  1. Elbow bent 20-30°; hold the upper arm still
  2. Valgus: push the forearm outward (tests the medial ligament)
  3. Varus: push it inward (tests the lateral ligament)
Positive

Pain or more gap than the other side

Tap to reveal

Tendons at the epicondyles

Lateral epicondylitis ("tennis elbow")

Wrist extensor tendons

Steps
  1. Elbow straight, fist made
  2. Resisted wrist extension (and resisted middle finger extension)
  3. Then passively bend the wrist down (stretch)
Positive

Pain at the lateral epicondyle

Tap to reveal

Medial epicondylitis ("golfer's elbow")

Wrist flexor tendons

Steps
  1. Elbow straight
  2. Resisted wrist flexion
  3. Then passively bend the wrist back (stretch)
Positive

Pain at the medial epicondyle

Tap to reveal

Steps
  1. Stand behind; hands on the iliac crests

Positive

Pelvis drops on the lifted-leg side = the standing side is weak

SI joint (or hip)

FABER test (Flexion, ABduction, External Rotation)

SI joint or hip

Steps
  1. Patient lies on their back
  2. Ankle on the opposite knee (figure-4)
  3. Hold the opposite pelvis (ASIS) and press the bent knee down
Positive

Back-of-pelvis pain = SI joint · groin pain = hip

Ober's test

Tight IT band

Steps

  2. Bend the top knee 90°; take the leg back and out
  3. Let it drop toward the table

Leg stays up (does not drop)

Leg length

True and apparent leg length

Leg length difference

Steps
  1. Patient lies straight
  2. True: tape from the ASIS to the medial malleolus
  3. Apparent: tape from the belly button to the medial malleolus
Positive

MCL (medial) / LCL (lateral)

  3. Varus: push it outward (tests lateral)

Pain or gapping vs the other knee

ACL (most accurate test)

  2. Hold the thigh still
  3. Pull the shin forward
Positive

More forward movement, soft end point

Anterior drawer

ACL

Steps

  2. Hands behind the top of the shin
  3. Pull the shin forward
Positive

More forward movement than the other side

Tap to reveal

Posterior drawer

PCL

Steps
  1. Same position as the anterior drawer, then push the shin backward
Positive

More backward movement

Posterior sag sign

Meniscus tear

Steps

  3. Lateral: turn the foot in, then straighten
Positive

Painful click or catch on the joint line

Thessaly's test

Meniscus tear

Steps

  2. Twists the body in and out 3 times
Positive

Joint-line pain, locking or catching

Morton's neuroma

Fracture: X-ray or not?

Ottawa ankle and foot rules

Who needs an X-ray (age 18 or over)

Steps
  1. Press the back edge and tip of each malleolus (distal 6 cm of tibia and fibula)
  2. Press the base of the 5th metatarsal and the navicular
  3. Ask them to walk 4 steps
Positive

Any bony tenderness there, or cannot bear weight for 4 steps (right after the injury and in the emergency department) → X-ray
