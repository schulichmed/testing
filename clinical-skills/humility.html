Cultural Humility

The interview is the same patient-centred spine as always (introduction → chief complaint → HPI with FIFE → past history → medications → habits → social → family → review of systems → closing summary), with two adaptations taught in this block: gender-expansive care, where you use the patient's correct pronouns and respectful language, acknowledge past negative experiences in health care, and validate their identity; and interviews in a language other than English, where you communicate through a family member translator and make sure the patient's own concerns are accurately conveyed and understood. One rule runs through both: humility means the patient is the expert on their own identity, culture and story, so you ask rather than assume, and you recognize and address your own implicit biases. Built from the Clinical Skills objectives for the Cultural Humility Communication Block and Advanced Interviewing, on top of the faculty Interviewing Template. The OneNote page "Gender Expansive Care & Language Other than English (LOE)" is empty (title only), so no faculty handout backs the detail here; points marked extra are kept to the few a beginner needs.

Look: everything shown.

1 The general approach

  • Say it first (TOSCE, Teaching Objective Structured Clinical Exam): "Hello, my name is [name], I'm a medical student working with Dr. [X]. What name would you like me to use, and what pronouns do you use?"
  • Build on the basics: apply the patient-centred interview from Introduction to Interviewing (effective communication, rapport, active listening) with a specific focus on equity, diversity and inclusion (EDI) and cultural competence
  • Respect difference: show awareness, understanding and respect for cultural differences and how they shape a person's view of health care, health promotion and disease management
  • Communicate inclusively: use inclusive and sensitive communication so patients from every background feel respected and understood
    • ask open questions; don't assume a person's gender, partners, family structure, beliefs or language from how they look
    • use FIFE: the Ideas and Expectations questions are where cultural beliefs about illness come out
  • Check yourself: recognize and address implicit bias, in yourself and in others, during the interaction
  • Stay professional: respect, reliability, honesty and clarity throughout

2 Gender-expansive carePoM 1 · Cultural Humility

  • Build rapport with a transgender patient, including acknowledging past negative experiences in the healthcare system
  • Ensure correct use of pronouns and respectful language
  • Engage in conversation that validates the patient's identity and experiences, creating a safe and inclusive environment
  • Conduct a respectful interview with a member of the LGBTQ+ (lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer and more) community, using appropriate terminology and a non-judgmental approach later: PoM 1 Advanced Interviewing

Name

The name the patient uses

Example question

"What name would you like me to use?"

Listen for =The name to use every time, and in what you tell the team

Pronouns

How to refer to the patient

Example question

"What pronouns do you use?"

Listen for =she/her, he/him, they/them or others; use them consistently

Past experiences of care

Trust and barriers

Example question

"Some people have had difficult experiences in health care. Is there anything that would make today more comfortable for you?"

Listen for =Being misgendered, dismissed or refused care before · what helps now

Gender-affirming care

Part of the medication and surgical history

Example question

"Are you taking any hormones, or have you had any surgeries, as part of your care?"

Listen for =Hormones (dose, who prescribes) · surgeries · asked like any other medication or operation

Sexual history

Partners and practices, without assumptions

Example question

"Are you currently sexually active? Tell me about your partners."

Listen for =The patient's own words for partners and practices · contraception, safer sex, previous infections

Support and safety

Social context

Example question

"Who do you have in your corner? Do you feel safe at home?"

Listen for =Supportive people or isolation, rejection, unsafe home
  • Ask about gender identity and bodies only where it matters for the visit, and say why you are asking extra.
  • If you use the wrong name or pronoun, apologize briefly, correct yourself, and carry on extra.
  • In the review of systems, the template's "partners: men, women or both" becomes an open question about partners, so no answer is assumed.

3 Interviews in a language other than EnglishPoM 1 · Cultural Humility

  • Conduct effective communication through a family member translator, ensuring the patient's concerns are accurately conveyed and understood
  • Show respect for the patient's cultural background, understand the context of their immigration experience, and address any unique health challenges they face
  • Use the same communication skills as always: plain language, avoid ambiguity and clarify, summarize periodically so the patient can confirm

Language

The language the patient is most comfortable in

Example question

"Which language would you like to use today?"

Listen for =Their preferred language, and how much English they're comfortable with

The patient's own words

Whether the message got through

Example question

"Can you ask her to tell me, in her own words, what worries her most?"

Listen for =An answer that matches the question, not a summary from the family member

Immigration experience

Context for health

Example question

"How long have you been in Canada? How has the move been for you?"

Listen for =How recently they arrived, separation from family, stress

Unique health challenges

Barriers to care

Example question

"Is it easy for you to see a doctor or get your medications here?"

Listen for =No family doctor, cost, transportation, language barriers

Beliefs about the illness

FIFE Ideas and Expectations

Example question

"What do you think is causing this? What would you hope we can do?"

Listen for =Cultural or family explanations, traditional remedies, what a good outcome looks like

Understanding at the end

Agreement on the plan

Example question

"Could you ask him to tell me back what we've decided?"

Listen for =The plan said back correctly in the patient's words
  • Speak to the patient, not the translator: look at the patient and use "you" extra.
  • Keep sentences short, one question at a time, so less is lost in translation extra.
  • ⚠ A family member may soften, filter or answer for the patient, and some topics (safety at home, sexual history, mood) are hard to discuss in front of family: if the answers sound filtered, find a way to ask privately or offer a professional interpreter extra.

4 Culture across the whole interviewadvanced: PoM 1 Advanced Interviewing

  • Discuss cultural competency, including the CHAT tool (named in the objectives; not in OneNote)
  • Demonstrate the ability to adapt medical care to cultural expectations
  • Show respect for cultural diversity and differences in shared decision-making: who the patient wants involved in decisions is part of their culture
  • Work towards understanding and supporting Indigenous patients (objective only; not in OneNote)
  • Conduct a gynecological history in a sensitive and professional manner: see the Obstetric and Gynecologic History summary note

5 Closing and reflectingPoM 1 · Cultural Humility

  • Close as always: summarize, ask "Is there anything else that feels important for me to know?", and check the patient (through the translator, if one is used) agrees with the plan
  • Reflect on the encounter with a focus on cultural humility, using feedback from peers, standardized patients (SPs) and facilitators, and plan how to improve
  • Give and receive constructive feedback on cultural competence and inclusive communication, and learn collaboratively in a diverse group
  • Ask yourself afterwards: what did I assume, and where did that assumption come from?
