Patient-Centred Interview

Every history runs on the same spine: introduction and consent → patient identification → chief complaint → history of presenting illness (HPI) with FIFE (Feelings, Ideas, Function, Expectations) → past history → medications and allergies → habits → social history → family history → review of systems (ROS) → closing summary. Two rules run through it: the patient's agenda comes first, without losing sight of yours, and the interview is organized, yet organic: keep to the order, but follow the patient's lead and use signposting (saying where you are going next) so the order never feels like a checklist. Built from the faculty Interviewing Template (Introduction to Interviewing, with handwritten session notes) and the Clinical Skills objectives. The Advanced Interviewing page in OneNote is empty (title only), so section 12 lists what that block's objectives ask for. Points marked (upper-year notes) fill gaps from upper-year templates.

1 The general approach

  • Say it first (TOSCE, Teaching Objective Structured Clinical Exam): "Hello, my name is [name], I'm a medical student working with Dr. [X]. I'd like to get to know you and hear about what's been going on. Is it okay if I ask you some questions today?"
  • Explain the notes: "I'll be taking a few notes as we talk so I can share them with the healthcare team and make sure your care best reflects your experience."
    • tell the patient what they say is confidential (shared only with the care team)
    • keep note taking seamless: it must not interfere with the conversation or rapport
  • Settle the patient: comfort first: lights, noise, seating
  • Confirm identity (patient identification): name and age, then the patient's gender as they describe it
  • Build rapport without words: eye contact, facial expressions, posture, positioning and movement, vocal expression (rate, volume and tone)
    • session note: stay smiling, stay conversational and empathetic
  • Communication skills: encourage the patient to tell their own story; listen without interrupting; facilitate responses with words and nods; avoid ambiguity and clarify; notice verbal and non-verbal cues (body language, speech, facial expression, affect); summarize periodically so the patient can confirm; use plain (lay) language that invites questions
    • ask why and how, then give the patient time to answer
    • follow up anything out of the ordinary
  • Professional behaviour: respect, reliability, honesty, courtesy, acceptance of difference, clarity, respecting patient–physician boundaries; non-judgmental; respect confidentiality, its limits, and autonomy
  • Rapport: accept the patient's views and feelings, use empathy, provide a supportive environment, offer partnership, deal with topics in a sensitive manner

2 Chief complaint and the patient's agendaFoM · Introduction to Interviewing

  • Open: "What brings you in today? What's the main thing you'd like us to focus on together?"
  • Get the whole agenda early: "Is there anything else on your mind that you'd like to make sure we cover?"
  • Address the patient-driven HPI first: let them lead before you take over with your questions
  • Respect the patient's agenda without losing sight of the physician's agenda: if the patient brings several concerns, agree together which to focus on today
  • With a patient who keeps drifting: "I'd like to use this time to focus on what brought you in today. Can you tell me more about…?" (upper-year notes)

3 History of presenting illnessFoM · Introduction to Interviewing

  • Open the story: "Tell me more about what's been happening with your health recently."
  • Then characterize each symptom. The template's order spells SOCRATES (sample questions are for pain):

Site

Where the problem is

Example question

"Where do you feel it most?"

Listen for =One spot or spread out; can they point to it

Onset

When and how it started

Example question

"When did you first notice it? Did anything happen?"

Listen for =Sudden vs gradual · a trigger (injury, illness, new medication)

Character

What it feels like

Example question

"Could you describe the pain?"

Listen for =Sharp, throbbing, burning, dull, pressure

Radiation

Spread

Example question

"Where else do you feel the pain?"

Listen for =Spread to the chest, shoulder, back or down a limb

Associated symptoms

Other symptoms that come with it

Example question

"When the pain is present, do you have any other symptoms?"

Listen for =Each one leads into a focused review of that system

Timeline

Duration and course

Example question

"Has it changed over time? Have you had similar pain in the past?"

Listen for =Escalating, steady or easing · previous episodes

Exacerbating / relieving factors

What changes it, including what they've tried

Example question

"Is there anything that makes it worse? Anything that makes it better?"

Listen for =Activity, food, position, rest · attempted treatments and whether they helped

Severity

How much it bothers them

Example question

"On a scale from 1 to 10, how much does this bother you?"

Listen for =The number, and what it stops them doing
  • If there is a history of similar episodes or flare-ups, ask about them compared with this one.
  • Recognize the importance of a thorough HPI: associated symptoms point you to the systems to review in detail.

4 The illness experience: FIFEFoM · Introduction to Interviewing

  • Understand the difference between disease (what the doctor finds) and illness (what the patient lives through)
  • Follow the patient's leads and explore their understanding of the illness from their own point of view, using the four FIFE dimensions:

Feelings

Emotions about the symptoms

Example question

"How do you feel about these symptoms?"

Listen for =Worry, fear, frustration, embarrassment

Ideas

The patient's own explanation

Example question

"What do you think might be happening?"

Listen for =A feared diagnosis (e.g. a relative had cancer) · cultural or family beliefs

Function

Impact on daily life

Example question

"Has this affected your daily life, like work, sleep, relationships, or things you enjoy?"

Listen for =Missed work, poor sleep, giving up activities

Expectations

What they want from today

Example question

"What are you hoping for today? Maybe answers, reassurance, or a treatment plan?"

Listen for =A test, a name for it, reassurance, a specific treatment
  • Recognize the patient's context and how the illness affects their work, family, social life and finances.

  • Take a medication history covering prescription and non-prescription medications, medication aids, adherence, barriers to taking medication and the patient's understanding of each medication

Prescriptions

Every medication

Example question

"Are you taking any prescription medications?"

Listen for =Name of each

Non-prescription

Over-the-counter (OTC), herbal, vitamins, supplements

Example question

"Do you take anything from the pharmacy or health-food store, like vitamins or supplements?"

Listen for =Products patients don't think of as "medicine"

Dose and frequency

How it is actually taken

Example question

"How often and what dose do you take?"

Listen for =Dose, how often, what time of day

Purpose

Patient's understanding

Example question

"What is it for?"

Listen for =Whether they know why they take it

Adherence and aids

Missed doses and why

Example question

"How often do you find you miss a dose? Do you use a pill box?"

Listen for =Cost, side effects, forgetting, confusion · blister packs or pill organizers

Allergies

Reactions

Example question

"Do you have any allergies? What happens when you're exposed?"

Listen for =The reaction (hives, chest tightness) · a true allergy vs an intolerance such as nausea (upper-year notes)
  • Maintain patient safety by educating about taking medications as prescribed.

Listen for =Cups per day

"Do you smoke or vape? How much a day, and for how many years?"

Listen for =Packs per day × years · interest in stopping (upper-year notes)

9 Family historyFoM · Introduction to Interviewing

  • Signpost: "Now on to your family history." Obtain a thorough family history
  • "Are there any family members with diabetes, heart disease, stroke, cancer, or mental health issues?"
  • For mother, father, siblings, children (and grandparents): age, health, and cause of death
  • Ask about the conditions relevant to the HPI, plus diabetes, heart attack, stroke, cancer, alcohol use, depression

10 Review of systemsFoM · Introduction to Interviewing

  • Introduce it: "We've talked through your main concerns. Just to be thorough, I'll ask a few quick questions about different parts of your health. Most of these can be yes or no. Stop me anytime if something stands out."
  • An anatomic approach: go system by system, head to toe; a thorough HPI pays particular attention to the ROS

General

Whole-body illness

Example question

"Any weight change without trying, tiredness, fevers or night sweats? Any changes in your skin, hair or nails?"

Listen for =Unintended weight loss, fatigue, fever, night sweats

Head, eyes, ears, nose, throat (HEENT)

Local symptoms

Example question

"How's your vision and hearing? Any ringing, dizziness, nosebleeds, sore throat or trouble swallowing?"

Listen for =Vision, hearing, tinnitus, vertigo, congestion, dysphagia (trouble swallowing), voice change

Respiratory

Lungs

Example question

"Any cough, wheezing, or coughing up phlegm or blood? Short of breath with activity or at rest?"

Listen for =Cough, sputum, hemoptysis (coughing blood), dyspnea (breathlessness), wheeze, chest pain

Cardiovascular

Heart and vessels

Example question

"Do you ever feel your heart racing or skipping beats? Have you fainted or felt close to passing out?"

Listen for =Chest pain, palpitations, syncope (fainting), sweating, breathless lying flat or waking at night, edema (swelling), leg pain on walking, varicose veins

Gastrointestinal

Gut

Example question

"Any nausea, vomiting or heartburn? Changes in your bowel movements, or blood or mucus in your stool? Any yellowing of your skin or eyes?"

Listen for =Abdominal pain, heartburn, bowel changes, blood in stool, bloating, jaundice, appetite change

Urologic

Urinary tract

Example question

"Any pain or burning when you pee? Going more often? Waking at night to urinate? Any blood in your urine? Any leaks when you cough or sneeze?"

Listen for =Dysuria, frequency, urgency, hematuria, hesitancy, incontinence, night-time voiding

Gynecologic and obstetric

Reproductive history

Example question

"When was your last period? Any changes in your cycle or bleeding between periods?"

Listen for =Menarche, cycle, spotting, last period, menopause, pelvic pain · past pregnancies and complications · if pregnant: bleeding, fluid leak, fetal movement

Sexual

Sexual health

Example question

"Are you currently sexually active? With men, women, or both? Any pain, discharge or sores?"

Listen for =Active now or ever, consensual, partners, practices, satisfaction, erectile problems, discharge, lesions, contraception, safer sex, previous sexually transmitted infections

Musculoskeletal

Joints and muscles

Example question

"Any joint pain, swelling, redness or stiffness? Any muscle aches, cramps or weakness?"

Listen for =Pain, swelling, redness, cramps, weakness

Neurologic

Nervous system

Example question

"Any frequent headaches? Numbness, tingling or weakness in your arms or legs? Trouble with memory or speaking? Any seizures or blackouts?"

Listen for =Headache, numbness, weakness, vision or speech change, memory change, confusion, seizures

Endocrine

Hormones

Example question

"Have you been more thirsty or urinating more than usual? Any unexplained sweating or change in appetite?"

Listen for =Heat or cold intolerance (thyroid, if tired), excess thirst, hunger and urination, sweating

Psychiatric

Mood and safety

Example question

"How has your mood been? Have you lost interest in things you normally enjoy? Have you ever had thoughts of harming yourself or others?"

Listen for =Low or elevated mood, anxiety, lost interest, guilt, hallucinations, history of abuse · ⚠ thoughts of self-harm or harming others
  • Normalize discussions of sexuality and sexual behaviour: ask everyone, in the same matter-of-fact way.
  • ⚠ Any yes to thoughts of harming themselves or others is followed up before you move on: ask about plans.

11 Closing the interviewFoM · Introduction to Interviewing

  • Summarize all key concerns back to the patient, so they can correct you and you catch anything missed; then follow up and probe more (there is time: the interview is about 25 minutes, no need to rush the end)
  • Check emotions: "We have spent some time talking about your physical health. Do you have any concerns about the way you feel emotionally?"
  • Open the door: "That's everything I wanted to cover today. Before we move to the physical exam, is there anything else that feels important for me to know or that you'd like to bring up?"
  • Thank them: "Thank you so much for helping me understand what you've been experiencing. It really helps me see what's been going on and think about how we can plan your care in a way that best supports you."
  • Lay the groundwork for finding common ground: understand the patient's agenda and clarify yours · explore possible causes and the illness experience in context · explain the problem and proposed management clearly · give the patient a chance to ask questions · enable mutual discussion · check that you agree
    • common ground on three things: the nature of the problems and priorities, the goals of treatment, and the roles of the doctor and the patient
  • Develop a therapeutic relationship that acknowledges the patient's concerns
