Psychiatric Interview

The interview runs identification → presenting complaint and history of presenting illness → psychiatric screen → past psychiatric history → medical history and medications → family history → personal and social history → wind down, formulation and plan, and the mental status exam (MSE) is gathered the whole way through, not as a separate step. Two rules run through it: the interview has two components, content (information, symptoms) and process (rapport, empathy), and without the second the patient will not share the first; and safety (suicide and homicide) is asked about in every psychiatric interview. Built from the PoM 2 psychiatry lectures (the psychiatric interview, the mental status exam, suicide) and the CS2 Psychiatry objectives. Points marked (upper-year notes) fill gaps from upper-year study notes.

Look: everything shown.

1 The general approach

  • Say it first (TOSCE, Teaching Objective Structured Clinical Exam): "Some of the questions I'll ask are personal. I ask them of everyone I see, and you can tell me if you'd rather not answer something."
  • Two components, held in balance
    • Content: information and symptoms, which build the diagnostic hypothesis
    • Process: rapport and empathy, the relationship that lets the patient trust you with sensitive information
  • Interview skills: use greeting, open-ended questions, effective listening, empathic comments, paraphrasing, summarizing and reassurance
    • funnel strategy: start open ("What brought you to the hospital today?"), then specific ("How is your anxiety interfering with your life?"), then closed, verifying questions ("Do you isolate yourself when depressed?")
    • empathic comment: "That sounds frightening." · paraphrase: "So the voices started after you stopped the medication?" · summarize at each change of section
  • Therapeutic alliance: build rapport while keeping appropriate boundaries (friendly, not a friend)
  • Transference and countertransference: recognize them and the effect they have on the patient-doctor relationship (upper-year notes)
    • transference: the patient unconsciously redirects feelings about someone from their past onto you (e.g. treats you like a dismissive parent)
    • countertransference: your own unconscious reaction to the patient (e.g. dreading a patient, or giving one who reminds you of a friend extra time)
  • Your own biases: recognize personal biases and keep them from interfering with the patient's best interests; afterwards, reflect on the interview for bias and transference/countertransference
  • Adjust to the situation: a focused, structured interview: adjust the history and MSE to the patient's complaint and the setting (an emergency needs safety first; a clinic follow-up needs the course of illness)

2 Identification and the history of presenting illness

  • Identifying data: name · age · gender (ask pronouns if not obvious) · marital and parental status (who is caring for the children now?) · occupation and finances · cultural background
  • Chief complaint in the patient's own words, then the history of presenting illness (HPI) with your hypotheses in mind

The problem, openly

The patient's story

Example question

"What difficulties are you experiencing today?"

Listen for =The main symptom group (mood, anxiety, psychosis, substances) to aim the rest of the interview

A reference point

Where "present" begins

Example question

"When were you last well? When were you content with how you were functioning?"

Listen for =A date or event: separates this episode from the past psychiatric history

Precipitants

What set it off

Example question

"Did anything change before this started?"

Listen for =Relationship difficulties · stopping treatment or a medication change · substance use · a medical illness or new medical drug

Impact

Severity

Example question

"How is this affecting your work, home and relationships?"

Listen for =Loss of function (stopped working, not leaving the house, not eating)

Coping and others

Supports, risk to others

Example question

"How have you been coping? How is this affecting the people around you?"

Listen for =Healthy coping vs using alcohol or drugs to cope (upper-year notes)

3 The psychiatric screenbefore the past psychiatric history

  • Screen every major group, even when the complaint points to one: a depressed patient may also be manic, anxious or drinking. This is how you recognize and screen for the major disorders: anxiety, mood, substance use, personality, psychotic and cognitive.

Depression

A major depressive episode

Example question

"Have you been feeling down or depressed, or lost interest in things, most of the day, nearly every day, for 2 weeks or more?"

Listen for =Low mood or loss of interest for 2 weeks or more · then SIGECAPS: Sleep, Interest, Guilt, Energy, Concentration, Appetite, Psychomotor change, Suicide (upper-year notes)

Mania or hypomania

Bipolar disorder

Example question

"Have you ever had a time when you felt so 'up' or full of energy that you got into trouble, or others said you weren't yourself?"

Listen for =Elevated or irritable mood (not while intoxicated) · less need for sleep · fast, pressured speech · racing thoughts · grandiosity · risky behaviour · ⚠ always ask about mania in anyone depressed (upper-year notes) · DIGFAST (3 or more, 4 if only irritable): Distractibility · Indiscretion (spending, sex) · Grandiosity · Flight of ideas · Activity up or agitation · Sleep need down · Talkative (pressured)

Anxiety

Generalized anxiety, panic, phobias, trauma

Example question

"Do you worry a lot about everyday things? Have you had sudden rushes of fear with a racing heart?"

Listen for =Hard-to-control worry · panic attacks peaking within minutes · avoidance · nightmares or flashbacks after a trauma · generalized anxiety: worry most days for 6 months or more, plus 3 of 6: restless (keyed up) · easily tired · poor concentration (mind going blank) · irritable · muscle tension · poor sleep (BE SKIM (upper-year notes))

Trauma

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)

Example question

"Has something very frightening or upsetting happened to you? Do you have frightening dreams about it, or flashbacks where it feels like it's happening again? Do you avoid places or thoughts that remind you of it?"

Listen for =Re-experiencing (nightmares, flashbacks) · avoidance · numbing, can't enjoy things · on edge · a month or more (TRAUMA (upper-year notes): Traumatic event · Re-experiencing · Avoidance · Unable to function · Month or more · Arousal)

Psychosis

Hallucinations, delusions

Example question

"Have you heard things other people couldn't hear, such as voices? Have you felt people were watching you or plotting against you?"

Listen for =Voices, visions · being followed, mind read, special messages · only when sad or high? (mood disorder with psychotic features)

Eating disorder

Anorexia, bulimia

Example question

"Are you preoccupied with your weight, or with food?"

Listen for =Restricting, bingeing, purging, fear of weight gain

Substance use

Substance use disorder

Example question

"How much alcohol do you drink in a week? Do you use cannabis or other drugs?"

Listen for =Use despite harm · tolerance · withdrawal · failed attempts to cut down · time spent getting or using · alcohol screens: AUDIT (10 items) is the better screen; CAGE (felt you should Cut down · Annoyed by criticism · Guilty · Eye-opener) is the quick one

Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD)

Obsessions, compulsions

Example question

"Do you have upsetting thoughts or urges that keep coming back? Do you feel driven to wash, check or count?"

Listen for =Intrusive, unwanted thoughts and repeated acts that relieve the anxiety

Safety

Suicide, self-harm, violence

Example question

"Have you had thoughts of ending your life, or of hurting someone else?"

Listen for =Any yes → full risk assessment (section 4)
  • Tentative diagnosis: apply the DSM-5 (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th edition) criteria to make a tentative diagnosis of anxiety, depression, psychosis, substance use, personality and cognitive disorders. The screen tells you which criteria set to check.
  • Normal sadness (e.g. bereavement) vs a mood disorder: grief comes in waves tied to reminders of the loss, with self-esteem preserved; a depressive episode is pervasive, with worthlessness, guilt and loss of function (upper-year notes).
  • Situational stress vs an anxiety disorder vs drugs or a medical condition: is the worry in proportion to a real stressor, and does it settle? Ask about caffeine, stimulants, alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal, and medical causes (e.g. thyroid) (upper-year notes).
  • Primary psychotic disorder vs other causes of psychosis: think substance intoxication or withdrawal and medical conditions (e.g. delirium) when onset is sudden, the patient is older, or hallucinations are visual (upper-year notes).
  • Long-enduring patterns vs short-lived episodes: a personality disorder is a pattern present since adolescence or early adulthood, across situations; mood and psychotic disorders come in episodes with a "last well" date (upper-year notes).

4 ⚠ Safety: suicide and homicide risk assessment

  • Ask directly and calmly. Be honest about why you ask, take the time to listen, validate the person's story and their decision to seek help, and stay non-judgmental.
  • If an answer doesn't fit the question, reframe the question: patients can misunderstand, or avoid.
  • Self-injury is not the same as suicidal ideation: explore what it does for the person. It is easily missed while looking for the bigger safety concerns.

Ideation

Thoughts of death or suicide

Example question

"Sometimes when people feel this low, they think about dying or ending their life. Has that happened to you?"

Listen for =Passive (no effort to think about it or plan) vs ⚠ active (thinking it through, planning, moving to act)

How it feels to them

Ego-syntonic vs ego-dystonic

Example question

"How do you feel about having these thoughts?"

Listen for =Dystonic: distressing, unwanted · ⚠ syntonic: fits their current values, not distressing (a red flag)

Plan

How far it has gone

Example question

"Have you thought about how you would do it? When, and where?"

Listen for =⚠ A specific plan: get the details (method, timing, place); don't assume

Access to means

Can they act on it

Example question

"Do you have access to pills, or to a firearm?"

Listen for =⚠ Seeking or having the means (stockpiled pills, a gun at home)

Intent

Will they act

Example question

"Do you think you would act on these thoughts?"

Listen for =⚠ Stated intent, or preparations (writing a note, giving things away) (upper-year notes)

Past attempts

The strongest history clue

Example question

"Have you ever tried to end your life? What happened?"

Listen for =Number, method and intent of each · what led up to it (the chain of events) · what saved them and how they felt about surviving · interrupted or aborted attempts

Warning signs

Imminent risk

Example question

"How hopeful do you feel about the future?"

Listen for =IS PATH WARM: Ideation · Substance use · Purposelessness · Anger · Trapped · ⚠ Hopelessness · Withdrawal (from people) · Anxiety · Recklessness · Mood changes

Protective factors

Reasons for living

Example question

"What has kept you going? What keeps you safe?"

Listen for =Strong relationships (family, community, culture, religion), belonging, future goals, willingness to accept help

Homicidal thoughts

Risk to others

Example question

"Have you had thoughts of hurting anyone else? Who?"

Listen for =Same approach as suicide: ideation, plan, means, intent, and who
  • ⚠ Red flags: active threats · talking about wanting to die · seeking access to means · hopelessness · unexpected talk or writing about death.
  • Risk is weighed from risk factors + warning signs + protective factors, and it is "not psychological math". In a severe-risk situation, protective factors may count for little.
  • ⚠ Decide whether the patient needs emergency care because of withdrawal symptoms, complications, violence, suicide or abuse, and act: stay with the patient, tell your supervisor, keep the setting safe.
    • inpatient vs outpatient depends on the acuity and severity of the risk and the safety net at home
    • in Ontario, any physician can complete a Form 1 (Application for Psychiatric Assessment) for an involuntary assessment when a mental disorder makes the person a danger to self or others (upper-year notes)
    • safety (crisis) plan: steps for a crisis · coping plan: strategies to stop stress escalating · ⚠ a "no-suicide contract" is not acceptable management
  • Collateral (family, family doctor) can confirm or challenge what you heard; privacy can be breached only when safety requires it, with care.

5 Past psychiatric history

  • Goals: the longitudinal course of the illness · its severity · the predisposing, precipitating and protective factors
  • The illness: age of onset · first symptoms and triggers · functioning before and after · number and length of hospitalizations
  • Impact: "How is your life different because of the illness?" · "How do you cope?" · "What opportunities were lost?"
  • Treatments
    • biological: medications (names, doses, how long) · electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) · repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) · bright light therapy (BLT)
    • psychosocial: which psychotherapy, and what strategies were learned
    • "Has anything made a difference?"
  • Add to the MSE: insight (does the patient appreciate their symptoms and what drives them?) and judgement (decisions that led to action: leaving an abusive relationship, starting therapy, getting sober)

6 Medical history, medications, family history

  • Medical history: ask for every patient, then target the medical problems each disorder brings

Mood disorders

Weight change

Example question

"Has your weight changed?"

Listen for =Weight loss (melancholic depression) · weight gain from carbohydrate craving (seasonal depression)

Schizophrenia, bipolar disorder

Metabolic disease

Example question

"Have you been told you have diabetes or high cholesterol?"

Listen for =Diabetes · dyslipidemia

Substance use

Complications of use

Example question

"Have you ever injected drugs? Been tested for HIV or hepatitis?"

Listen for =Skin lesions (crystal methamphetamine) · abscesses, HIV, hepatitis B and C (injection) · alcoholic pancreatitis

OCD

Skin damage

Example question

"Do your hands get sore from washing?"

Listen for =Excoriation (picking), maceration from handwashing

Family history

Inherited and environmental risk

Example question

"Has anyone in your biological family had mental health problems, problems with alcohol or drugs, or attempted suicide?"

Listen for =Mental illness · substance use · eccentric behaviour · suicide attempts or deaths · estrangement
  • Medications and allergies, including over-the-counter and herbal remedies. A recent medication change can be the precipitant.
  • Non-biological relatives still matter: an alcoholic step-parent shapes the environment, not the genes.

7 Personal and social history

  • Go chronologically, from childhood, once rapport exists, keeping your hypothesis in mind and showing genuine interest.

Development

Early environment

Example question

"What was home like growing up? Who raised you?"

Listen for =Pregnancy and birth problems (briefly) · two or single parent · attachment (closeness, instability) · clear, consistent family rules

Childhood adversity

Abuse, neglect

Example question

"Were your needs met? Did you ever see violence at home? Were you ever touched in a way that made you uncomfortable?"

Listen for =Violence, punishment out of proportion, sexual abuse

School

Learning, bullying

Example question

"How did you do in school? Any difficult experiences, like being bullied?"

Listen for =Special classes, repeated grades, bullying

Personality

Lifelong traits

Example question

"What were you like as a child: outgoing, or shy and quiet?"

Listen for =Interests, friendships, enduring patterns

Relationships and supports

Social support

Example question

"Are you in a relationship now? Who can you count on?"

Listen for =Pattern across relationships · current support (relatives, friends, community)

Sexual history

Sexual health

Example question

"Are you attracted to men, women, or both? What protection do you use?"

Listen for =Orientation, safer sex, past STIs (sexually transmitted infections), sexual difficulties

Legal history

Legal problems

Example question

"Have you ever been charged with an offence, or been on probation?"

Listen for =Charges, convictions, probation or parole, driving offences, upcoming court dates

Occupation

Function over time

Example question

"What is your main line of work? What's the longest you've stayed in a job?"

Listen for =Education, job history, current work situation

8 The mental status exam

  • Mostly an objective description gathered by observation during the interview (not a separate part), then reported as its own heading in the case presentation. Components: ABC STAMP LICKER.
  • Identify and report common MSE findings in the major disorders (last column).

Appearance

Identifying features

How to assess

Watch from the first moment

Abnormal =Dress wrong for the weather or setting, poor grooming or hygiene (depression, psychosis) · flamboyant (mania)

Behaviour

Activity, eye contact, movements

How to assess

Observe throughout

Abnormal =Slowed (depression) · restless, agitated (mania, anxiety, withdrawal) · tremor, stereotypies (purposeless repeated acts), posturing

Cooperation

Willingness to engage

How to assess

How the interview goes

Abnormal =Argumentative, evasive, guarded (paranoia) · accessibility: is the patient willing and able to share?

Speech

The "pulse" of the MSE

How to assess

Listen: rate, rhythm, tone, volume, latency (delay before answering)

Abnormal =Pressured (a need to speak: mania) · poverty of speech (few words) · poverty of content (many words, little information) · mutism, dysarthria

Thought form

How thoughts flow

How to assess

From the speech

Abnormal =Circumstantial (detours, gets there) · tangential (never gets there) · flight of ideas (fast jumps: mania) · derailing (abrupt unconnected change) · loosening of associations (no logical link: psychosis)

Thought content

What the patient thinks about

How to assess

Ask about worries and beliefs

Abnormal =Delusions (fixed false beliefs out of keeping with the patient's culture: persecutory, grandiose, reference, nihilistic, jealousy, erotomanic) · overvalued ideas · obsessions, phobias

Affect

Emotion shown in the moment (objective)

How to assess

Watch the face and tone

Abnormal =Restricted (to one feeling, e.g. sad) · flat (schizophrenia) · inappropriate to the content · incongruent with the stated mood

Mood

The patient's state over time (subjective)

How to assess

"How would you describe your mood lately?", in their words

Abnormal =Depressed · euphoric · irritable · anxious

Perception

Hallucinations, illusions

How to assess

"Do you ever hear or see things others don't?"

Abnormal =Hallucination (perception with no stimulus; auditory commonest: command, running commentary) · illusion (a real stimulus misperceived) · pseudohallucination (experienced "in the mind's eye or ear")

Level of consciousness

Alertness

How to assess

Observe

Abnormal =Drowsy, fluctuating (delirium, intoxication)

Insight and judgement

Not the same thing

How to assess

"What do you think is going on? What would help?"

Abnormal =Poor insight: doesn't see the illness or the need for treatment · poor judgement: can't weigh consequences (focus on illness-related choices)

Cognition

Orientation, attention, memory, executive function

How to assess

Person, place, date; months backwards; recall; MoCA if concerned (section 9)

Abnormal =Disoriented, inattentive (delirium) · memory loss (dementia)

Knowledge and intelligence

What they know, and what they do with it

How to assess

From vocabulary and answers

Abnormal =Below what education predicts

Endings: safety

Suicidal and homicidal thoughts

How to assess

Always ask (section 4)

Abnormal =Passive vs active, ego-syntonic, plan, intent

Reliability

Can you trust the history?

How to assess

Consistent with other data and within the interview?

Abnormal =Inconsistencies, gaps · get collateral
  • Mood is the climate, affect is the weather. Report mood in the patient's own words.
  • Hallucinations are not always illness: on falling asleep or waking (hypnagogic, hypnopompic), or with poor vision (Charles Bonnet syndrome).
  • Phenomenology: describe how the patient experiences a symptom, not just its name. "Auditory hallucinations" doesn't tell you what the voices are like: ask the volume, whose voice, what they say, whether the patient talks back.

9 Cognitive screen: the Montreal Cognitive AssessmentMoCA

  • Demonstrate skill in using the MoCA: about 10 minutes, out of 30; below 26 suggests cognitive impairment. It catches mild cognitive impairment better than the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE).
  • Read the instructions exactly as written and don't give hints. Make sure glasses and hearing aids are on.

Visuospatial and executive

Planning, drawing

How to assess

Trail making (1-A-2-B…) · copy the cube · draw a clock (contour, numbers, hands)

Abnormal =Errors (1 + 1 + 3 points)

Naming

Language

How to assess

Name 3 animals (lion, rhinoceros, camel)

Abnormal =Cannot name them (3 points)

Memory: learning

Registration

How to assess

Read 5 words, twice

Abnormal =No points yet: recalled later

Attention

Concentration

How to assess

Digits forward and backward · tap at each letter A · serial 7s from 100

Abnormal =Errors (2 + 1 + 3 points)

Language

Repetition, fluency

How to assess

Repeat 2 sentences · words starting with one letter in 1 minute

Abnormal =Fewer than 11 words (2 + 1 points)

Abstraction

Abstract thinking

How to assess

"How are a train and a bicycle alike?"

Abnormal =Concrete answers (2 points)

Delayed recall

Short-term memory

How to assess

The 5 words, with no cues

Abnormal =Missed words (5 points)

Orientation

Time and place

How to assess

Date, month, year, day, place, city

Abnormal =Errors (6 points)
  • The score is influenced by age and education: the MoCA instructions add 1 point for 12 years of education or less extra.
  • ⚠ Don't use the MoCA in delirium (acute, fluctuating confusion).

10 Winding down, formulation and plan

  • Winding down: don't finish on a sensitive topic · thank the patient · acknowledge their commitment · ask if anything was missed · ask FIFE: the patient's Feelings, Ideas, Function and Expectations about the problem · ask how they feel now · discuss the plan
  • Formulation: the biological, psychological and social factors that predisposed the patient, and those that protected them
  • Plan: a biopsychosocial initial treatment plan adapted to the patient
    • most urgent next steps · how to deal with safety issues · inpatient or outpatient
    • biological (e.g. medication) · psychological supports and therapy · social (housing, work, supports)
  • Explain it: communicate the working diagnosis and plan in clear, plain language the patient understands; find common ground when you disagree
  • Refer: help patients access the system for physical, psychological, social and economic rehabilitation or long-term care (e.g. community mental health, addiction services, social work)
  • Limits: seek help or consultation when needed, and accept you can't be "all things to all people": defer some requests, kindly.
  • Present the case: summarize the clinical, biological and psychosocial information to the facilitator and peers, clearly and concisely: identification, chief complaint, HPI, pertinent screen positives and negatives, past history, MSE, risk assessment, tentative diagnosis, plan. High-yield discriminators:
  • ⭐ Content needs process: without rapport and empathy the patient won't share. Funnel open → closed; set a reference point ("When were you last well?").
  • ⚠ Safety every time: suicide and homicide · passive vs active · plan, means, intent · hopelessness is a key warning sign.
  • Mood = subjective, over time (patient's words) · affect = objective, in the moment (what you see).
  • Insight ≠ judgement: insight is appreciating the illness; judgement is the decisions that follow.
  • Always screen for mania in depression; think substances and medical causes before calling psychosis or anxiety primary.
  • MoCA < 26 suggests impairment; not in delirium.
