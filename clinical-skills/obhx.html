Obstetric and Gynecologic History

The history runs the usual way, presenting complaint → gynecologic symptoms → menstrual history → obstetric history → past history → medications → family → social → review of systems → summary and FIFE (Feelings, Ideas, Function, Expectations), with the Ontario Perinatal Record (OPR) as the checklist for a pregnant patient. Two rules run through it: these are private questions, so explain why you are asking and ask without judgment, and ask about pregnancy losses gently: the patient chooses how much to share. Built from the faculty OB/Gyne History Gathering Reference (2024) and the Ontario Perinatal Record (2017). Points marked (upper-year notes) fill gaps from upper-year study notes.

Look: everything shown.

1 The general approach

  • Say it first (TOSCE, Teaching Objective Structured Clinical Exam): "Some of the questions I'll ask are personal. I ask them of every patient because they help me understand what's going on. You can skip any question you'd rather not answer."
  • Open the interview: open in a way that creates a professional and trusting setting, so the patient can discuss their concerns openly
    • introduce yourself and your role, confirm the patient's name and pronouns, make sure the door is closed
    • establish rapport and keep it through the whole interview
  • Notice context: identify contextual factors that may shape how you and the patient see things: history of personal violence, sexual orientation, body image, gender, cultural expectations, language and literacy
    • offer an interpreter when needed (the OPR has a box for "Interpreter required")
    • never assume the gender of a partner
  • Watch the patient: recognize non-verbal cues (pauses, looking away, tearfulness) that hint at how the patient feels about the complaint, and name them gently: "This seems hard to talk about."
  • Presenting complaint first: use open-ended questions to let the patient describe the main concern, then ask about its impact on daily life and what they are doing about it now
    • character and site · timing (onset, duration, cyclical, intermittent or continuous) · what makes it better or worse · previous episodes, investigations and treatments · associated symptoms
  • Then tailor: gather the patient's obstetric and gynecologic background, and tailor the rest of the history to what the complaint needs
  • Close: summary, FIFE, a plan in plain words (section 7)

2 Gynecologic symptomsPoM 2 · OB/Gyn history

  • After the presenting complaint, screen for the common symptoms of gynecologic disease, even if the patient did not mention them.

Vaginal bleeding

Abnormal bleeding

Example question

"Have you had any bleeding between periods, after sex, or after your periods stopped for good?"

Listen for =Intermenstrual (between periods) · post-coital (after sex) · ⚠ post-menopausal (after menopause)

Abdominal or pelvic pain

The character of the pain

Example question

"Can you show me where the pain is? When did it start?"

Listen for =SOCRATES: Site · Onset · Character · Radiation · Associated symptoms · Time course · Exacerbating and relieving factors · Severity

Vaginal discharge

Infection

Example question

"Has your discharge changed recently?"

Listen for =A change in colour, consistency, amount or odour (discharge itself is usually normal)

Dyspareunia (painful sex)

Where the pain is

Example question

"Is sex ever painful? Is the pain at the opening or deeper inside?"

Listen for =Superficial (introital), at the opening on entry · deep, felt deep in the pelvis

Vulvar itching, skin changes

Skin and infection problems

Example question

"Any itching, lumps or skin changes around the vulva or anus?"

Listen for =Vulvar itch · anogenital skin changes

Infertility

Difficulty conceiving

Example question

"How long have you been trying to get pregnant?"

Listen for =Duration · investigations so far · assisted conception attempts
  • ⚠ Bleeding after menopause is the one never to let pass (upper-year notes): it is endometrial cancer until proven otherwise, though atrophic vaginitis is the most common cause.

Age at the first period

Past problems

Long-standing patterns

Example question

"Have your periods ever been irregular, very heavy, or very painful?"

Last menstrual period (LMP)

Dating, possible pregnancy

Example question

"When did your last period start? Was it a normal period for you?"

Listen for =The first day of bleeding or spotting · ⚠ in early pregnancy an implantation bleed can be mistaken for a period, so ask if it was normal

Listen for =Date

Cycle length and regularity

Irregular cycles

Example question

"How many days from the start of one period to the start of the next? Is that the same each month?"

Listen for =Cycle length = LMP to the previous period · regularity = the pattern over the past year

Days of bleeding

Heavy or prolonged periods

Example question

"How many days do you usually bleed?"

Listen for =Number of days

How heavy

Heavy menstrual bleeding

Example question

"On your heaviest days, how often do you change your pad or tampon?"

Listen for =Heavy: changing more often than every 3 hours, 21 or more pads/tampons a cycle, changing overnight, bleeding through clothes or bedding, clots larger than 1 inch, missing work or school, told they are anemic

Current bleeding problems

Abnormal bleeding now

Example question

"Any heavy bleeding, bleeding between periods, or bleeding after sex lately?"

Listen for =Current or recent heavy, intermenstrual or post-coital bleeding

Pain and premenstrual symptoms

Dysmenorrhea, premenstrual syndrome

Example question

"Do you get symptoms in the days before your period?"

Listen for =Mood swings, irritability, bloating, headaches, acne, cravings

Postmenopausal patients

Menopause history

Example question

"How old were you at your last period? Any bleeding or spotting since?"

Listen for =Age at last period · hormone therapy · ⚠ any bleeding after menopause
  • A normal period: pad or tampon changed every 3 hours or longer, fewer than 21 per cycle, seldom changed overnight, clots under 1 inch, not anemic.

4 Obstetric history

  • Start easy: "How many children have you given birth to?" Then sensitively ask about miscarriages, stillbirths, ectopic pregnancies and terminations.
    • explain why you ask, validate ("That must have been very hard"), and let the patient decide how much to say (upper-year notes)
    • some patients have not told their current partner about past pregnancies: ask privately
  • Summarize as GTPAL (the OPR's "Pregnancy Summary"): Gravida = all pregnancies, including the current one · Term = delivered at 37 weeks or more · Preterm = delivered 20 to 37 weeks · Abortus = losses before 20 weeks (miscarriage, ectopic, termination) · Living children. The OPR also counts stillbirths and neonatal or child deaths.
    • twins count as one G but two L
  • Then go pregnancy by pregnancy: the OPR's obstetrical history chart has one row per pregnancy: year, place of birth, gestation, labour length, sex, birth weight, type of birth, breastfeeding, the child's current health, and comments.

Gestation

Preterm birth

Listen for =⚠ Previous preterm labour raises the risk of preterm labour again

Mode of delivery

Type of birth

Example question

"Was it a vaginal birth or a Caesarean section?"

Listen for =Spontaneous vaginal · assisted vaginal (vacuum, forceps) · Caesarean

Sex and birth weight

Growth problems

Example question

"Do you remember how much the baby weighed?"

Listen for =⚠ A previous small for gestational age (SGA) baby raises the risk of another

Complications

Problems likely to recur

Example question

"Were there any problems during the pregnancy, the birth, or afterwards?"

Listen for =Pre-eclampsia · gestational hypertension · gestational diabetes · obstetric anal sphincter injury (3rd or 4th degree tear) · postpartum hemorrhage

Assisted reproduction (ART)

How the pregnancy began

Example question

"Did you need any help getting pregnant?"

Listen for =Ovulation induction (e.g. clomiphene) · in vitro fertilization (IVF)

Care providers

Higher-risk past care

Example question

"Did a midwife look after you, or did you see an obstetrician?"

Listen for =Midwife only, or obstetric input, and why

Earlier losses

Pregnancies that ended early

Example question

"Would you be comfortable telling me about any pregnancies that ended early?"

Listen for =Gestation, miscarriage or termination, any cause found for a miscarriage or stillbirth

Ectopic pregnancy

Pregnancy outside the uterus

Example question

"Where was the ectopic, and how was it treated?"

Listen for =Site · management: expectant (watching), medical (methotrexate injection), surgical (laparoscopy or laparotomy; removing or opening the tube)
  • The faculty reference details each pregnancy carried beyond 24 weeks and groups those under 24 weeks with the losses; the GTPAL counts above use the 20-week cut-off from PoM 2 and the OPR.

5 Past history, medications, family and social history

  • Cervical screening: date of the last screening test (Pap/HPV test), the result, and any treatment that followed. If the patient has not been screened, ask gently why: a bad experience or past abuse may be the reason (upper-year notes).
  • Past history: pelvic or abdominal surgery · past gynecologic problems (e.g. fibroids, endometriosis) · sexually transmitted infections (STIs)
  • Sexual history (upper-year notes): currently active? partners (number and gender), practices, protection, past STIs, pregnancy prevention, any pain or bleeding

Contraception

Pregnancy risk, side effects

Example question

"Are you using any birth control? How often do you miss a pill?"

Listen for =Type and brand · compliance / correct use · previous methods

Hormone replacement therapy (HRT)

Bleeding and cancer risk

Example question

"Are you taking any hormones for menopause?"

Listen for =Duration · cyclical or continuous · combined or estrogen-only · how it is taken (pill, patch, gel)

Other medications

Drug effects

Example question

"Any other prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins or herbal remedies?"

Listen for =Prescription, over-the-counter, herbal · some antibiotics cause vaginal yeast infection (candidiasis)

Allergies

Drug reactions

Example question

"Any allergies? What happens when you take it?"

Listen for =Reaction and severity

Immunizations

Prevention

Example question

"Have you had the HPV (human papillomavirus) vaccine?"

Listen for =HPV vaccine

Family history

Inherited risk

Example question

"Has anyone in your family had breast, ovarian or uterine cancer?"

Listen for =Breast, ovarian, endometrial cancer (can run in families, e.g. BRCA1/2) · diabetes (linked to some reproductive problems) · bleeding disorders (cause heavy periods)

Weight and diet

Hormonal effects, deficiencies

Example question

"Has your weight changed? Any diet restrictions?"

Listen for =Rapid weight loss (eating disorder) → missed or rare periods · obesity → menstrual changes, endometrial cancer risk, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) · vegan or vegetarian → B12, iron, vitamin D deficiency

Occupation, home, finances

Exposures, support

Example question

"What do you do for work? Who do you live with?"

Listen for =Industrial exposures · living arrangements, independence, financial security

Substance use

Health risks

Example question

"Do you smoke, vape, drink alcohol or use cannabis or other drugs?"

Listen for =Nicotine, cannabis, alcohol, caffeine, other substances
  • Review of systems: especially urinary and bowel symptoms, fever (e.g. pelvic inflammatory disease, PID), fatigue (anemia from heavy periods), ⚠ weight loss and abdominal distension (malignancy).
  • Screen for intimate partner violence, abuse and high-risk sexual activity with empathy and respect, asking when the patient is alone (upper-year notes).

6 The pregnant patient: the Ontario Perinatal RecordPoM 2 · OB/Gyn history

  • Use the Ontario Perinatal Record as your guide for a comprehensive obstetric history. Work through it in its order; it doubles as your checklist.
  • Identify and date the pregnancy: LMP, cycle length and regularity, contraceptive type and when last used, planned pregnancy?, assisted conception? (IUI, intrauterine insemination, or embryo transfer dates), estimated date of birth (EDB) by LMP, by first- or second-trimester ultrasound (T1US, T2US), and the final EDB
    • Naegele's rule (upper-year notes): EDB = LMP + 7 days − 3 months + 1 year
  • How do you feel about this pregnancy? (upper-year notes): excited, anxious or unplanned

Early complications

"Since you became pregnant, have you had any bleeding, nausea or vomiting, a rash or fever?"

Listen for =Bleeding · nausea/vomiting · rash, fever or illness

Nutrition

Deficiencies, neural tube defects

Example question

"Did you take folic acid before you got pregnant? Are you on a prenatal vitamin?"

Listen for =Calcium and vitamin D adequate · folic acid preconception · prenatal vitamin · food access and quality · dietary restrictions

Medical and surgical history

Conditions that change care

Example question

"Do you have any medical conditions, or have you had surgery or a problem with an anaesthetic?"

Listen for =Hypertension · cardiac/pulmonary · endocrine (diabetes, thyroid) · GI/liver · breast · gynecologic · urinary · rheumatologic · hematologic · thromboembolic/clotting · blood transfusion · neurologic · anaesthetic complications

Genetic history

Inherited conditions in the baby

Example question

"What is your ethnic background, and your partner's? Does anything run in either family?"

Listen for =Ethnic background of egg and sperm (and age) → carrier screening (hemoglobinopathies; Tay-Sachs; Ashkenazi Jewish panel) · genetic conditions (e.g. cystic fibrosis, muscular dystrophy, chromosomal disorder) · intellectual disability, birth defects, congenital heart disease, recurrent loss, stillbirth · consanguinity (partners related by blood)

Infectious disease

Infections that harm the baby

Example question

"Have you had chickenpox or the vaccine? Have you or your partner ever had herpes?"

Listen for =Varicella (disease or vaccine) · HIV · HSV (herpes simplex virus), self and partner · STIs · at-risk for hepatitis C, tuberculosis, parvovirus, toxoplasmosis

Mental health

Perinatal anxiety and depression

Example question

"Over the last 2 weeks, how often have you felt nervous or on edge? Had little interest or pleasure in doing things?"

Listen for =Anxiety and depression, past and present · GAD-2 (Generalized Anxiety Disorder) score of 3 or more or PHQ-2 (Patient Health Questionnaire) score of 3 or more → assess further · eating disorder, bipolar, schizophrenia, other (PTSD, ADD)

Substance use

Fetal exposure

Example question

"Have you smoked in the last 6 months? Do you ever drink alcohol? When was your last drink?"

Listen for =Cigarettes per day · drinks per week and T-ACE score of 2 or more (risk; needs follow-up) · cannabis · non-prescribed drugs

Lifestyle and social

Safety and support

Example question

"Do you feel safe at home? Who will support you after the baby comes?"

Listen for =Occupational risks · financial or housing problems · poor social support · beliefs or practices affecting care · relationship problems · ⚠ intimate partner or family violence · parenting concerns
  • T-ACE (alcohol): Tolerance (needs more than 2 drinks to feel high = 1 point) · Annoyed by criticism · Cut down · Eye-opener (yes = 1 point each).
  • Also record the family physician, the planned birth attendant and place of birth, and the newborn care provider.

7 Wrapping up: summary, FIFE, differential and plan

  • Summarize: read back what you heard and ask for corrections; it often prompts the patient to add something important.
  • FIFE: ask about the patient's Feelings, Ideas, Function and Expectations. It guides the discussion of next steps.
  • Differential: formulate and prioritize a differential diagnosis and a preliminary management plan from what you heard
    • ⚠ post-menopausal bleeding: endometrial cancer until proven otherwise (upper-year notes)
    • ⚠ bleeding in pregnancy: first trimester, think miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, molar pregnancy or an implantation bleed; third trimester, think placenta previa (painless) or placental abruption (painful) (upper-year notes)
  • Plan in plain words: answer questions in lay terms and give a clear plan of care, checking the patient understands and agrees
  • Present it: summarize the encounter to present to peers and faculty: identifying data and GTPAL, the presenting complaint, pertinent positives and negatives, your differential and plan.
