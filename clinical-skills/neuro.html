Neurological exam

The neurological exam runs from the top down: mental status → cranial nerves → motor (bulk, tone, power) → sensory (primary, then cortical) → reflexes → coordination → gait and balance. Two rules run through all of it: compare left with right (power is tested one side at a time), and every finding helps answer "where is the lesion?", above all whether it is an upper motor neuron (brain or spinal cord) or a lower motor neuron (nerve root, nerve or muscle) problem. OneNote holds only her typed motor notes for Weeks 8 and 9 and an empty Year 2 Neuro page, so most of this note is built from the course objectives, her older neuro exam notes and upper-year notes; anything only the PoM 2 Neurology session teaches is marked advanced.

Look: everything shown.

1 The general approach

  • Say it first (TOSCE, Teaching Objective Structured Clinical Exam): "Hi, I'm [name], a medical student. I'd like to examine your nervous system: I'll test your eyes and face, the strength, feeling and reflexes in your arms and legs, your coordination and your walking. Is that all right? Tell me if anything is uncomfortable."
  • Prepare: introduce yourself, check the patient's identity, get consent, privacy, clean your hands before and after contact, warm hands, personal protective equipment (PPE) as needed
  • Equipment: penlight, eye chart, ophthalmoscope, cotton wisp, tuning forks (128 Hz for vibration, 512 Hz for hearing), reflex hammer, a sharp and a dull object
  • Explain each step in plain words and demonstrate on an area you are not testing
  • Order: mental status → cranial nerves → motor → sensory → reflexes → coordination → gait
  • Compare sides, and proximal (near the trunk) with distal (far)

2 Mental statusPoM 1 · PPES 2 Weeks 8–9

  • Appearance and conduct: dress, grooming, hygiene, posture, behaviour, any involuntary movements or obvious paralysis
  • While interviewing, screen orientation, memory and judgment
    • level of consciousness and orientation: alert? oriented to person, place and time?
    • attention: can they focus, answer questions and follow directions?
    • mood and affect: voice, facial expression, demeanour; ask how their mood has been
    • speech and language: clarity, amount, rate, fluency, comprehension, reading and writing
    • thought content: perceptions, judgment, memory
  • Folstein Mini-Mental State Exam (MMSE): perform, score and interpret later: PoM 2 Neurology: not in her OneNote or the upper-year notes; use the session's own form
  • Types of dysphasia (aphasia) later: PoM 2 Neurology: aphasia is a problem with language, not with moving the mouth (that is dysarthria); language lives in the dominant hemisphere (usually the left)
    • expressive (Broca's): inferior frontal lobe · slow, halting, effortful speech, few words or none · understanding intact · usually aware of the problem
    • receptive (Wernicke's): posterior temporal lobe · fluent speech that does not make sense · poor understanding · often unaware of the problem
    • conduction (arcuate fasciculus): understands and can speak, but cannot repeat
  • Types of parietal lobe syndrome later: PoM 2 Neurology:
    • dominant (left) parietal: Gerstmann syndrome: right-left confusion, cannot write (agraphia), cannot calculate (acalculia), finger and object recognition problems
    • non-dominant (right) parietal: neglect: ignores one side of space or the body (visual or sensory), denies the deficit (anosognosia), cannot draw or build things (constructional apraxia)
    • apraxia: cannot carry out a movement on command (pretend to use a hammer, wave) despite normal power
    • cortical sensory loss (see section 5): primary sensation intact but stereognosis or graphesthesia lost

3 Cranial nervesPoM 1 · PPES 2 Weeks 8–9

  • Examine cranial nerves I to XII in an organized, logical order later: PoM 2 Neurology; PPES 2 starts at II
  • The eye, ear, mouth and face tests are the same moves as the head and neck exam
  • Look at the face and eyes at rest first: symmetry, drooping lid, pupil size

CN I, olfactory: smell later: PoM 2 Neurology

Loss of smell

Steps
  1. Patient closes the eyes and blocks one nostril
  2. Offer a familiar, non-irritating smell (coffee, soap)
  3. Ask them to name it
  4. Repeat on the other nostril
Positive =Cannot smell or identify it, one side or both (normal: identifies it each side)

CN II, optic: visual acuity

Visual acuity

Steps
  1. Distance glasses on (note them)
  2. Cover one eye; read the smallest line they can on the chart
  3. Repeat with the other eye
Positive =Reduced acuity in one or both eyes (normal: 20/20)

CN II: visual fields by confrontation

Visual fields

Steps
  1. Eye level, about an arm's length apart
  2. Patient covers one eye, you close your opposite eye; they look at your nose
  3. Bring a finger in from the edge in all four quadrants (or hold up fingers to count)
  4. Repeat for the other eye
Positive =Missed quadrant or field, or a blind spot (scotoma) (normal: sees the finger when you do)

CN II and III: pupillary light reflex

Pupillary light reflex (II carries light in, III constricts the pupil)

Steps
  1. Note pupil size, shape, symmetry
  2. Patient looks into the distance
  3. Light into one eye: watch direct and consensual constriction
  4. Repeat on the other eye
  5. Near reaction: follow your finger in toward the nose
Positive =Unequal, irregular, sluggish or absent response (normal: PERRLA, pupils equal, round, reactive to light and accommodation)

CN II: fundoscopy

Optic disc and retina

Steps
  1. Dim the room, ophthalmoscope dial at 0
  2. Approach from about 15° to the side; see the red reflex
  3. Follow a vessel to the optic disc
  4. Note disc margins, colour, cup-to-disc ratio, then vessels, retina and macula
Positive =Swollen disc with blurred margins (papilledema), haemorrhages (normal: sharp margins, pale pink to yellow disc, cup less than half the disc, veins larger and darker than arteries)

CN III, IV, VI: eye movements

Extraocular movement

Steps
  1. Finger 45–60 cm away; head still, eyes follow
  2. Trace an "H"
  3. Ask about double vision
  4. Watch for nystagmus at the ends
Positive =Restricted movement, double vision, misalignment or nystagmus (normal: full movement, no diplopia)

CN V, trigeminal: sensation

Sensory function of the face

Steps
  1. Patient closes the eyes
  2. Touch a cotton wisp to the forehead (V1), cheek (V2) and chin (V3) on each side
  3. Ask whether each side feels the same
Positive =Reduced or absent feeling in one branch or one side (normal: equal on both sides)

CN V: motor

Motor function (muscles of chewing)

Steps
  1. Feel the temporalis (temple) and masseter (jaw angle) on both sides
  2. Patient clenches the teeth
Positive =Weak or uneven contraction, wasting (normal: strong and equal)

CN V and VII: corneal reflex

The V and VII reflex: V carries the touch in, VII closes the eye

Steps
  1. Patient looks up and away
  2. Come in from the side, out of view
  3. Lightly touch the cornea with a cotton wisp
  4. Repeat on the other eye
Positive =No blink (normal: both eyes blink)

CN VII, facial: motor

Motor function of facial expression

Steps
  1. Raise the eyebrows
  2. Close the eyes tightly against your attempt to open them
  3. Smile, bare the teeth
  4. Puff out the cheeks
Positive =One side weak: whole side = lower motor neuron (for example Bell's palsy); lower face on the opposite side only, forehead spared = upper motor neuron (for example stroke) (normal: symmetric, strong)

CN VII: sensory

Sensory function: taste, front two-thirds of the tongue

Steps

1. Ask about any change in taste

Positive =Taste loss on one side of the front of the tongue

CN VIII, vestibulocochlear: hearing

Hearing

Steps
  1. Block one ear; whisper numbers or rub your fingers by the other, out of sight
  2. If abnormal: Weber (512 Hz fork on top of the head) and Rinne (fork on the mastoid, then beside the ear)
Positive =Weber louder in the bad ear and Rinne bone louder than air = conductive · Weber louder in the good ear = sensorineural (normal: hears whisper; Weber central; Rinne air louder than bone)

CN IX and X: palate

Motor function

Steps
  1. Listen to the voice and ask about swallowing
  2. Patient says "ahh"
  3. Watch the soft palate and uvula
Positive =Palate rises on one side only, uvula pulled to one side; hoarse voice (normal: symmetric rise, uvula midline)

CN IX and X: gag reflex

Gag reflex

Steps
  1. Tell the patient first
  2. Lightly touch the back of the throat on each side with a tongue depressor
Positive =No gag on one side or both (normal: gag on each side); not done routinely, mostly to check airway protection or the brainstem

CN XI, spinal accessory

Motor function of the trapezius and sternocleidomastoid

Steps
  1. Look for wasting or asymmetry of the neck and shoulders
  2. Shrug against your resistance
  3. Turn the head to each side against your hand
Positive =Weakness or wasting on one side (normal: strong, equal)

CN XII, hypoglossal

Motor function of the tongue

Steps
  1. Look at the tongue resting in the mouth: wasting, fasciculations
  2. Stick the tongue straight out
  3. Move it side to side; push it into each cheek against your finger
Positive =Tongue deviates toward the weak side, wasting, fasciculations, weak push (normal: midline, strong)
  • The gag and corneal reflexes are in the course objectives but are not done on every patient; know how and why.
  • To say: "Cranial nerves II to XII are intact."

4 Motor: bulk, tone, powerPoM 1 · PPES 2 Weeks 8–9

  • Watch movements first: smooth movement, normal rate and rhythm, no tremor
  • Muscle bulk: inspect for atrophy (wasting), hypertrophy and asymmetry
    • fasciculations later: PoM 2 Neurology: small, spontaneous twitches of part of a muscle, a lower motor neuron sign
  • Muscle tone: the resistance you feel when you move a relaxed limb yourself
    • patient goes floppy; move the limb passively (wrist, elbow, knee) slowly, then quickly
    • normal, reduced (hypotonia, floppy), or increased (hypertonia: spastic or rigid)
    • spasticity vs rigidity later: PoM 2 Neurology:
      • spasticity (upper motor neuron): resistance depends on speed (worse the faster you move); clasp-knife: resistance builds, then suddenly gives way
      • rigidity (basal ganglia, for example Parkinson disease): resistance the same at any speed, from tightening of opposing muscles together; lead-pipe = smooth and even throughout · cogwheel = jerky, ratchet-like; it gets more obvious when the patient moves the other hand (augmentation)
  • Muscle power: test one side at a time and compare; against gravity, then against full resistance, graded out of 5
  • Power grading, Medical Research Council (MRC) scale; naming the root and nerve for each movement (myotomes) is later: PoM 2 Neurology:
    • 0 no movement
    • 1 a flicker of contraction
    • 2 moves with gravity eliminated
    • 3 moves against gravity, no added resistance
    • 4 against some resistance (4−, 4, 4+ show how much)
    • 5 full power

Shoulder abduction

Shoulder power: C5, axillary nerve (deltoid, supraspinatus)

Steps
  1. Arms out to the sides, elbows bent
  2. "Don't let me push down"
  3. Push down above the elbow
Positive =Weakness, one side or both (normal: 5/5)

Elbow flexion

Elbow power: C5–C6, musculocutaneous nerve (biceps, brachialis)

Steps
  1. Elbow bent 90°, palm up
  2. "Pull me toward you"
  3. Resist at the wrist
Positive =Weakness

Elbow extension

Elbow power: C6–C8 (mainly C7), radial nerve (triceps)

Steps
  1. Elbow bent
  2. "Push me away"
  3. Resist at the wrist
Positive =Weakness

Finger abduction

Finger power: C8–T1, ulnar nerve (interossei)

Steps
  1. Fingers spread wide
  2. "Don't let me push them together"
  3. Squeeze the index and little fingers in
Positive =Weakness

Grip strength

Grip: screens C6–T1

Steps
  1. Patient squeezes two of your fingers
  2. Compare sides
Positive =Weak grip

Hip flexion

Hip power: L2–L4, femoral nerve (iliopsoas)

Steps
  1. Seated or lying, lift the thigh
  2. Push down on the thigh
Positive =Weakness

Knee extension

Knee power: L2–L4, femoral nerve (quadriceps)

Steps
  1. Knee bent
  2. "Kick out straight"
  3. Resist at the shin
Positive =Weakness

Knee flexion

Knee power: L4–S2, sciatic nerve (hamstrings)

Steps
  1. Knee bent
  2. "Pull your heel back"
  3. Resist at the back of the ankle
Positive =Weakness

Ankle dorsiflexion

Ankle power: L4–L5, deep peroneal nerve (tibialis anterior)

Steps
  1. "Pull your foot up toward your face"
  2. Resist on top of the foot
Positive =Weakness (foot drop)

Ankle plantar flexion

Ankle power: S1, tibial nerve (gastrocnemius, soleus)

Steps
  1. "Push your foot down like on a gas pedal"
  2. Resist under the ball of the foot
Positive =Weakness
  • Upper vs lower motor neuron lesion later: PoM 2 Neurology:
    • upper motor neuron (brain or spinal cord): weakness in a pyramidal pattern (arm extensors and leg flexors weaker), increased tone (spasticity), brisk reflexes, clonus (repeated rhythmic beats), upgoing toe (Babinski sign), little wasting, no fasciculations
    • lower motor neuron (anterior horn cell, root, nerve): wasting, fasciculations, reduced tone (flaccid), reduced or absent reflexes, toe down or no response
    • just after a sudden upper motor neuron lesion (stroke, cord injury) the limb can be floppy at first, with spasticity developing over the following days to a week
  • To say: "Bulk and tone are normal; power is 5 out of 5 throughout."

5 SensoryPoM 1 · PPES 2 Weeks 8–9

  • Explain and demonstrate on a normal area first (for example the forehead), then test with the patient's eyes closed
  • Ask "Does this feel the same as this?" side to side and distal to proximal; it is the pattern of loss that localizes
  • Two pathways, two sets of modalities later: PoM 2 Neurology:
    • spinothalamic tract: pain and temperature (and crude touch); fibres cross within a level or two of entering the cord
    • dorsal (posterior) columns: vibration and joint position (proprioception) (and fine touch); fibres stay on the same side until the brainstem
  • Posterior column vs spinothalamic lesions later: PoM 2 Neurology:
    • posterior cord lesion: lost vibration and proprioception, pain and temperature kept (for example vitamin B12 deficiency, syphilis (tabes dorsalis)); positive Romberg (section 8)
    • anterior cord lesion: lost pain and temperature and power below the level, vibration and proprioception kept
    • central cord: pain and temperature lost in a band ("suspended"), hands weaker than shoulders
    • half the cord (Brown-Séquard): same side weakness and lost vibration and proprioception · opposite side lost pain and temperature
    • a sensory level (normal above a line, reduced below) points to the spinal cord
  • Key dermatomes later: PoM 2 Neurology: C5 lateral shoulder and arm · C6 thumb and index finger · C7 middle finger · C8 ring and little finger · T1 medial arm · T10 umbilicus · L4 medial leg · L5 lateral leg and top of the foot · S1 lateral foot and sole

Pain (pinprick)

Pain (spinothalamic)

Steps
  1. Use a clean sharp object and a dull one
  2. Patient says "sharp" or "dull"
  3. Compare sides and distal with proximal
Positive =Cannot tell sharp, or feels it less, in a pattern (normal: equal, correct each time)

Temperature

Temperature (spinothalamic)

Steps
  1. Touch the skin with a cool tuning fork
  2. Ask "hot or cold?" and "is it the same as here?"
Positive =Reduced or absent sense of cold

Light touch

Light touch

Steps
  1. Cotton wisp, no pressure
  2. Patient says "yes" each time they feel it
  3. Compare areas
Positive =Missed touches in a pattern

Vibration

Vibration (dorsal columns)

Steps
  1. Strike a 128 Hz fork; show it on the forehead
  2. Place it on the last joint of the index finger (your finger underneath) and the big toe joint
  3. Patient says when they feel it and when it stops
  4. If lost, move up to the next bony point (start distally, move proximally)
Positive =Feels it stop early, or not at all, usually at the toes first (normal: feels it until you do)

Proprioception (joint position)

Proprioception (dorsal columns)

Steps
  1. Hold the end of the finger or big toe by its sides
  2. Show "up" and "down"
  3. Eyes closed: move it up or down in a random order
Positive =Wrong answers (normal: correct each time)

Stereognosis

Cortical sensation

Steps
  1. Eyes closed
  2. Put a familiar object (coin, key) in the hand
  3. Ask them to name it
Positive =Cannot name it although primary sensation is intact → parietal (sensory cortex)

Graphesthesia

Cortical sensation

Steps
  1. Eyes closed
  2. Draw a number on the palm with a blunt tip
  3. Ask them to name it
Positive =Cannot name it although primary sensation is intact → parietal (sensory cortex)
  • Primary sensory loss (pinprick, temperature, vibration) can come from anywhere along the pathway; only cortical sensory loss with intact primary sensation points to the cortex.

6 ReflexesPoM 1 · PPES 2 Weeks 8–9

  • Deep tendon and plantar reflexes, and the nerve roots of each
  • Patient relaxed with the limb properly positioned; let the hammer swing
  • Note the grade (amplitude) and symmetry; grade each reflex later: PoM 2 Neurology: 2+ is normal; brisk reflexes point to an upper motor neuron lesion, reduced or absent to a lower motor neuron lesion. The full grading scale is not in her OneNote or the upper-year notes: use the session's own scale.
  • Reinforcement if a reflex will not come: arms: clench the teeth (or spell a word backwards) · legs: hook the hands together and pull them apart, then tap

Biceps

C5–C6 (mainly C5)

Steps
  1. Arm relaxed, elbow bent
  2. Thumb on the biceps tendon in the elbow crease
  3. Tap your thumb
Positive =Absent, reduced, very brisk or unequal (normal: elbow flexes, equal both sides)

Brachioradialis

C5–C6 (mainly C6)

Steps
  1. Forearm resting, thumb up
  2. Tap the thumb side of the forearm, a few centimetres above the wrist
Positive =Absent, reduced, very brisk or unequal (normal: forearm flexes and turns slightly)

Triceps

C6–C7 (mainly C7)

Steps
  1. Arm across the body or hanging, elbow bent
  2. Tap the triceps tendon just above the point of the elbow
Positive =Absent, reduced, very brisk or unequal (normal: elbow extends)

Knee (patellar)

L2–L4 (mainly L4)

Steps
  1. Patient seated, legs dangling (or knees supported)
  2. Tap the tendon just below the kneecap
Positive =Absent, reduced, very brisk or unequal (normal: knee extends)

Ankle (Achilles)

S1–S2 (mainly S1)

Steps
  1. Foot gently pushed up (dorsiflexed)
  2. Tap the Achilles tendon above the heel
Positive =Absent, reduced, very brisk or unequal (normal: foot plantar flexes)

Plantar response

L5–S1, upper motor neuron sign (not a tendon reflex)

Steps
  1. With a blunt point, stroke the outer edge of the sole from the heel toward the toes
  2. Then across the ball of the foot
Positive =Big toe goes up and the other toes fan out (Babinski sign) = upper motor neuron lesion (normal: toes curl down)
  • To say: "Reflexes are 2+ and symmetrical; plantar responses are flexor (down-going) on both sides."

7 Coordination (cerebellum)PoM 1 · PPES 2 Weeks 8–9

  • Test cerebellar function: also listen to the speech (slurred, irregular) and watch the eye movements (nystagmus)
  • Cerebellar signs appear on the same side as the lesion

Rapid alternating movements

Cerebellar function: dysdiadochokinesia

Steps
  1. Hands: patient flips one hand palm up, palm down on the other palm or thigh as fast as they can
  2. Feet: taps the foot on the floor or your hand quickly
  3. Each side in turn
Positive =Slow, clumsy, irregular alternation (dysdiadochokinesia) (normal: fast, even rhythm)

Finger-nose

Cerebellar function: dysmetria and intention tremor

Steps
  1. Hold your finger at arm's length
  2. Patient touches your finger, then their nose, back and forth
  3. Move your finger between touches
Positive =Overshoots or misses (dysmetria), tremor worse near the target (intention tremor) (normal: smooth and accurate)

Heel-shin

Cerebellar function in the legs

Steps
  1. Patient lying down
  2. Place one heel on the opposite knee
  3. Slide it down the shin to the ankle, and back
  4. Each side
Positive =Heel wobbles or falls off the shin (normal: smooth, stays on the shin)

8 Gait and balancePoM 1 · PPES 2 Weeks 8–9

  • Gait: watch the patient walk: posture, balance, arm swing, stride, turning
  • Tandem gait (heel to toe in a straight line): the most sensitive screen for ataxia (unsteady, poorly coordinated walking)
  • Walk on the heels (ankle dorsiflexion strength) and on the toes (plantar flexion strength)
  • Cerebellar gait: wide-based, staggering, swaying while standing; falls toward the side of a one-sided lesion
  • Reduced arm swing, small steps and a stooped posture suggest Parkinson disease
  • Perform and interpret Romberg's test later: PoM 2 Neurology: balance needs two of three inputs (joint position sense, the vestibular system, vision); closing the eyes takes vision away

Tandem gait

Ataxia

Steps
  1. Walk in a straight line heel to toe
  2. Stand close by
Positive =Cannot keep the line, steps off or sways (normal: steady)

Romberg test later: PoM 2 Neurology

Sensory ataxia (lost joint position sense) vs cerebellar ataxia

Steps
  1. Patient stands with feet together, eyes open
  2. Stand close to catch them
  3. Patient closes the eyes (up to about 60 seconds)
  4. Watch for swaying or falling
Positive =Steady with eyes open but sways or falls with eyes closed = positive Romberg = sensory ataxia (dorsal columns or nerves: B12 deficiency, peripheral neuropathy) · unsteady even with eyes open = cerebellar ataxia (Romberg not positive) (normal: steady both ways)

9 Putting it together: where is the lesion?PoM 2 · Neurology

  • Explain and justify where the lesion is in the nervous system later: PoM 2 Neurology:
    • one side of the body plus the face or cranial nerves → brain
    • all four limbs (quadriparesis) → cervical spinal cord
    • both legs (paraparesis) → thoracic spinal cord or lumbar roots
    • weakness only in one root's muscles (one myotome) → nerve root
    • one limb, several nerves → plexus
  • Then ask upper or lower motor neuron (section 4) and which sensory pathway (section 5)
  • ⚠ New weakness, a sensory level, or bladder and bowel symptoms point to the spinal cord and need urgent assessment
