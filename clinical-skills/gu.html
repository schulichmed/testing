Male Genital and Rectal Exam

The exam runs consent and chaperone → patient standing: penis → scrotum and testes → inguinal region and hernia check → patient lying on their side: anus, then rectum and prostate → privacy to dress, then explain the findings. Each part is look, then feel, comparing left with right. Two rules run through every step: say what you are about to do before you do it, and the patient can stop the exam at any time. There is no faculty material in OneNote for this session yet, and the upper-year notes cover only the hernia check (standing, examiner seated in front), so this note is written to the course objectives at a beginner level.

Look: everything shown.

1 The general approach

  • Say it first (TOSCE, Teaching Objective Structured Clinical Exam): "I'll explain each step before I do it. Let me know if anything is uncomfortable, and you can ask me to stop at any time."
  • Consent: explain why the exam is needed and what it involves (looking at and feeling the genitals, checking for a hernia, and a gloved finger in the rectum), and get the patient's agreement
  • Chaperone: offer one (many settings require one) and introduce them
  • Prepare: warm hands, gloves on for every part, privacy to undress, and a drape so only the area being examined is uncovered
  • Order: patient standing for the penis, scrotum and hernia check (hernias and varicoceles show best standing); then lying on their side for the rectal exam
  • Explain, then touch: name each step, use neutral words, watch the patient's face and check in
  • Stop means stop: if the patient asks you to stop, stop straight away

2 Normal anatomyT2C · Genitourinary

  • Understand normal male genital and rectal anatomy
  • Penis: the shaft (spongy erectile tissue, with the urethra running along the underside), the glans (the tip), the urethral opening (meatus) at the tip of the glans, and the foreskin (prepuce), which covers the glans if the patient is uncircumcised
  • Scrotum: two compartments, each holding
    • a testis: smooth, firm, rubbery, egg-shaped, about 15-25 mL in an adult; the left usually hangs lower
    • the epididymis: a soft comma-shaped ridge behind and on top of the testis
    • the spermatic cord: runs up from the testis to the inguinal canal; contains the vas deferens (a firm cord, like a piece of string) and blood vessels
  • Inguinal region: the inguinal canal runs just above and parallel to the inguinal ligament (from the hip bone, ASIS, to the pubic bone); its outer opening (external inguinal ring) sits just above and beside the pubic bone, where the spermatic cord leaves the abdomen
  • Rectum and prostate: the anal canal (about 4 cm) opens into the rectum; the prostate lies in front of the rectum (toward the bladder), felt through the front wall
    • normal prostate: about walnut-sized, two lobes with a groove down the middle (median sulcus), smooth and rubbery, not tender

3 PenisT2C · Genitourinary

  • Inspect the penis and describe its appearance, recognizing the spectrum of normal anatomy and disease; palpate to assess its structures

Inspection

Skin, hair, foreskin, meatus

Steps
  1. Look at the pubic hair and skin
  2. Look at the shaft and glans
  3. If uncircumcised, ask the patient to pull back the foreskin (or do it gently), then return it afterwards
  4. Look at the position of the urethral opening
Positive =Ulcers, warts, rashes, lice · discharge from the meatus · meatus on the underside or top of the shaft rather than the tip · foreskin too tight to pull back, or ⚠ stuck behind the glans (normal: clean skin, meatus at the tip, foreskin retracts and returns easily)

Palpation

Structure of the shaft

Steps
  1. Feel along the shaft between thumb and two fingers
  2. Gently squeeze the glans to open the meatus and look for discharge
Positive =Tender areas, hard lumps or plaques in the shaft · discharge (normal: soft, smooth, non-tender)
  • ⚠ A foreskin left pulled back can swell and get stuck: always return it at the end.

4 Scrotum and testes

  • Inspect the scrotum and palpate the scrotal contents: one side at a time, comparing left with right

Inspection

Shape, size, skin

Steps
  1. Lift the scrotum to see the back of it
  2. Compare the two sides
Positive =Swelling · redness · rashes, cysts, ulcers · ⚠ a testis sitting high or lying horizontally (normal: two sides roughly equal, left lower)

Testicular palpation

Testes

Steps
  1. Hold the testis gently between thumb and first two fingers
  2. Feel its size, shape, consistency and any lumps
  3. Repeat on the other side
Positive =⚠ A firm lump within the testis (testicular cancer until proven otherwise) · tenderness · one testis missing from the scrotum (normal: smooth, rubbery, equal, mildly sensitive)

Epididymis

Epididymis

Steps

1. Feel behind and on top of each testis

Positive =Tender swelling (epididymitis) · a separate cyst (normal: soft, non-tender ridge)

Spermatic cord

Cord and vas deferens

Steps
  1. Follow the cord up from the epididymis to the inguinal ring between thumb and finger
  2. Roll the vas deferens between your fingers
Positive ="Bag of worms" above the testis (varicocele, enlarged veins; usually left, best felt standing) · thickening or tenderness (normal: smooth cord with a firm string-like vas)

Transillumination extra

Whether a swelling is fluid or solid

Steps
  1. Darken the room
  2. Shine a penlight from behind the swelling
Positive =Glows red = fluid (hydrocele) · does not glow = solid (consider a tumour)
  • Cremasteric reflex extra: stroking the inner thigh makes the testis on that side rise; ⚠ it is often absent in testicular torsion (twisting of the testis, a surgical emergency).

5 Inguinal region and hernia check

  • Inspect to recognize inguinal hernias when present; palpate using the Valsalva maneuver (bearing down) to bring out small hernias
  • Patient standing; you sit or kneel in front (upper-year notes)

Inspection

Visible hernia

Steps
  1. Look at both groins at rest
  2. Ask the patient to bear down as if having a bowel movement (Valsalva) or cough
  3. Watch both groins and the scrotum
Positive =A bulge in the groin or scrotum that appears or grows with straining (normal: no bulge)

Palpation of the inguinal canal

Small hernia

Steps
  1. Use the index finger on the same side (right for right)
  2. Start low on the scrotum and push the loose scrotal skin up with your finger, folding it inward along the spermatic cord to the external inguinal ring (just above and beside the pubic bone)
  3. Ask the patient to bear down or cough
  4. Repeat on the other side
Positive =A bulge or impulse pushing against your fingertip or the side of your finger when the patient strains (normal: nothing felt, or the ring just tightens)

Inguinal lymph nodes (upper-year notes)

Enlarged nodes

Steps

1. With the pads of your fingers, roll gently over the skin above and below the inguinal ligament

Positive =Enlarged, hard or tender nodes (infection or cancer in the legs, genitals or anus) (normal: small, soft, mobile nodes can be felt)
  • ⚠ A hernia that is tender, firm and cannot be pushed back may be trapped (incarcerated): it needs urgent attention.

6 Anus, rectum and prostate

  • Inspect to recognize the spectrum of superficial anal disease; palpate and describe the prostate
  • Position: patient lies on their side (usually the left), knees drawn up to the chest, buttocks at the edge of the bed; a drape covers everything but the anus
  • Tell the patient: "You'll feel my finger now. It may feel like you need to have a bowel movement; that's normal."

Inspection of the anus

Superficial anal disease

Steps
  1. Gently spread the buttocks
  2. Look at the skin around the anus
  3. Ask the patient to bear down to bring out anything hidden
Positive =Hemorrhoids (swollen veins) · skin tags · fissure (a split in the skin) · fistula opening · warts · rash · bleeding (normal: puckered, darker skin, nothing protruding)

Digital rectal exam (DRE)

Anal tone, rectal wall

Steps
  1. Lubricate the gloved index finger
  2. Place the pad of the finger on the anus and wait for the muscle to relax
  3. Slowly insert it, pointing toward the belly button
  4. Ask the patient to squeeze around your finger (anal tone)
  5. Sweep around the rectal walls
Positive =Weak squeeze (poor anal tone) · a mass in the rectal wall · tenderness (normal: firm squeeze, smooth walls)

Prostate

Size, consistency, surface, tenderness

Steps
  1. Turn the finger to the front wall (toward the bladder)
  2. Feel both lobes and the groove between them
  3. Note size, consistency, surface and tenderness
Positive =Enlarged, smooth, rubbery, groove lost (benign enlargement, BPH) · ⚠ hard nodule or irregular, hard gland (prostate cancer) · very tender, boggy (prostatitis) (normal: walnut-sized, smooth, rubbery, two lobes with a groove, non-tender)

Withdrawing

Blood, stool

Steps
  1. Withdraw slowly
  2. Look at the glove
Positive =Blood (fresh red or black) or mucus (normal: brown stool, no blood)
  • Finish kindly: offer tissues to wipe, give privacy to dress, then explain what you found and what happens next.
