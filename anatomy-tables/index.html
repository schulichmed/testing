Clinical notes

Medial epicondylitis (golfer's elbow)

tendinopathy of common flexor origin

Flexor digitorum profundus

Continues as lumbricals

Lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow)

tendinopathy of common extensor origin (especially of extensor carpi radialis brevis)

Synovial cyst of wrist (ganglion cyst)

often on dorsum of wrist, connected with synovial sheaths of extensor tendons

de Quervain's tenosynovitis

inflammation of abductor pollicis longus & extensor pollicis brevis tendons at radial styloid process → test with Finkelstein's test (make fist with thumb inside and ulnarly deviate)

Snuff box

scaphoid fracture pain, radial nerve

Mallet finger (baseball finger)

sudden hyperflexion of finger (jamming) causes avulsion/partial tearing of extensor digitorum tendon from insertion on base of distal phalanx → unable to extend distal interphalangeal joint (DIP)

Clinical notes

Abductor pollicis brevis

Proximal median nerve lesionsHand of benediction when trying to make fist: median nerve compression in cubital fossa or between 2 heads of pronator teres → loss of flexion of proximal interphalangeal joints (PIPs)/distal interphalangeal joints (DIPs)/metacarpophalangeal joints (MPs) of digits 2-3

Abductor pollicis brevis

Distal median nerve lesionCarpal tunnel syndrome (flexor retinaculum over flexor pollicis longus (FPL)/flexor digitorum superficialis (FDS)/flexor digitorum profundus (FDP)/median nerve): median nerve compression in carpal tunnel → paresthesia of lateral 3.5 digits, paralysis/wasting of thenar muscles and lateral 2 lumbricals; test via Tinel's sign of wrist

Abductor pollicis brevis

Proximal ulnar nerve lesionsClaw hand: compression posterior to medial epicondyle or between 2 heads of flexor carpi ulnaris; lower brachial plexus injuries Klumpke paralysis C8-T1 → clawing of digits 4-5 (hyperextended MPs, cannot flex DIPs/extend interphalangeal joints (IPs) of digits 4-5)Waiter's tip arm position: upper brachial plexus injuries Erb-Duchenne palsy C5-6 → adduct shoulder, medially rotate arm, extend elbow; paresthesia lateral upper limb

Abductor pollicis brevis

Distal ulnar nerve lesion

Ulnar canal syndrome

ulnar nerve compression in Guyon's canal → decreased sensation of medial 1.5 digits, weak intrinsic hand muscles; clawing of digits 4-5 possible but flexion unaffected and no radial deviation

Opponens pollicis

Good test of recurrent branch of median nerve

Lumbricals

Extends off flexor digitorum profundus tendons (split innervation follows as well)

Adductor pollicis

Test by tugging at paper squeezed between digits 1/2

Palmar interossei

test by tugging at paper squeezed between adjacent digits 2-5

Dorsal interossei

test spreading digits 2-3 apart against resistance

Biceps femoris

All originate from ischial tuberosity, except short head (SH) of biceps femoris which originates from linea aspera

Biceps femoris

Semitendinosus inserts into pes anserinus while semimembranosus inserts into tibial medial condyle

Groin pull

tearing as result of active contraction/passive stretch of medial muscles close to origin, common in sports requiring quick starts/extreme stretching (soccer, sprints, gymnastics)

Anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS) avulsion

strong powerful hip flexion can avulse ASIS, which is origin of sartorius and tensor fascia latae, common in teens with weak cartilaginous growth plates relative to muscles/tendons

Adductor canal

transmits femoral artery and vein to adductor hiatus; formed by sartorius, vastus medialis, adductor longus

Adductor hiatus

opening between adductor magnus adductor part distal attachment and hamstring part tendon; transmits femoral artery and vein from anterior thigh to popliteal fossa, becoming popliteal artery and vein

Adductor brevis

Straddled by anterior + posterior divisions of obturator nerve

Gracilis

Disposable (weak adductor) → used for transplants (e.g. anterior cruciate ligament (ACL)/anal sphincter/facial reconstruction)

Shin splint

microtrauma to tibial periosteum where anterior leg muscles attach (especially tibialis anterior), usually result of overexertion/improper warm up or cool down, mild form of anterior compartment syndrome

Common peroneal nerve injury and footdrop
