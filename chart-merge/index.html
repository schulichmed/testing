Merging the note charts with the flowcharts

Every lecture note opens with an overview chart: one column per diagnosis, covering how it presents, its causes, the tests and the treatment. Ashish's flowcharts cover the other half, the route to a diagnosis: what to ask first and which test splits the possibilities. Below are five ways to put the two together, all using the same thyroid example (the chart from Approach to Hypothyroidism and Thyrotoxicosis and the flowcharts Abnormal TSH and Cause of thyrotoxicosis).

What we'd like from you, in the team chat:
  1. Which letter you would ship, and which you'd never use.
  2. Try it on your phone too. Does your pick still work there?
  3. Anything that confused you, or that you clicked expecting something to happen.
A Stacked B Tabs C Linked D Side by side E Walk-through
A

Stacked

The chart first, then the flowcharts underneath, as separate pictures. This is what we get by simply adding the flowcharts to the note.

Good: no new ideas to learn, works everywhere. Weak: nothing connects them, and the note gets long.

B

Tabs

One frame at the top of the note. The chart shows by default; the flowcharts are a tap away.

Good: the note stays short. Weak: you can't see both at once, and some people never open a tab.

C

Linked

Both stay on the page, but they point at each other. Click a dashed diagnosis box in the flowchart and the page jumps to that disease's column in the chart. Click a column in the chart and it jumps back with the route to that diagnosis lit up.

Good: the flowchart becomes a way into the chart. Weak: lots of jumping up and down on a phone.

Dashed boxes link to the chart.

Overview chart
D

Side by side

The flowchart on the left. Click a dashed diagnosis and just that disease's column from the chart opens beside it. The full chart is still in the note, further down.

Good: the route and the disease side by side, without scrolling. Weak: needs a wide screen; on a phone the card drops below.

Click a dashed diagnosis box to see its column from the chart.
E

Walk-through

One question at a time, using Ashish's questions and answers. When you reach a diagnosis you get its column from the chart, and the flowchart beside it shows the route you took.

Good: active practice, closest to how a case is asked. Weak: slower if you only want to look something up.
